## 01 · Dark psychology #16 · "Mixed signals over text"
You're getting mixed signals from a girl over text, warm at noon and short by night. [pause] [lower voice] Listen, there are usually three reasons.
Psychology calls it ambivalence. She can like you and still feel unsure, busy, or not ready.
So she might be busy. She might be unsure. Or she might be keeping you as an option.
[confident] Here's how to tell them apart.
Number one. Don't judge her words. Judge her follow-through.
Number two. Ask one clear thing. "Want to get coffee Saturday?"
Number three. If she says yes and picks a time, it's real. If she dodges twice, you have your answer.
You don't have to decode her mood. You only have to give her something she can say yes or no to.
[lower voice] Send this to the friend who keeps asking what her texts mean.

## 02 · Dark psychology #17 · "She acts uninterested"
If you think she likes you but acts uninterested, stop guessing and watch one thing. [pause] [lower voice] Listen, because there are two very different versions of this.
Psychology calls the first one self-protection. Some people play it cool so a no hurts less.
The other version is plain disinterest, and from the outside they look almost the same.
[confident] Here's the difference.
When she's protecting herself, she still replies, she remembers small things you said, and she makes time when you ask.
When she's not interested, the answers stay short, she never asks you anything back, and no plan ever gets made.
So test it once. Ask her out in plain words and watch what she does with it.
Effort tells you more than her tone ever will.
[lower voice] Save this for the next time she feels hard to read.

## 03 · Dark psychology #18 · "Signs she's losing interest"
Don't wait for slow replies, because the signs she is losing interest through text show up earlier. [pause] [lower voice] Listen, they're quiet.
Psychology calls it a drop in reciprocity. When someone cares less, she puts in less, one small piece at a time.
It doesn't prove she's gone. It does tell you the chat is cooling.
[confident] Here's what to look for.
Number one. Her replies get shorter than they were.
Number two. She stops asking you questions back.
Number three. She stops bringing up plans or things she'll do.
If you see two of these, don't send more texts to win her back.
Send one honest message instead. "I've liked talking to you. Want to do something this week?"
Her answer shows if there's still something to build.
[lower voice] Send this to the friend whose chat just went flat.

## 04 · Dark psychology #19 · "She's busy"
A girl says she's busy but interested, and most guys answer it the wrong way. [pause] [lower voice] Listen, here's the scene.
She texts, "This week is crazy, but I really want to see you."
He writes back, "No worries, just tell me when you're free."
[confident] Freeze.
That reply sounds relaxed, but it hands her the whole job. A busy person with a full week rarely plans something on her own, so the date just sits there.
Psychology calls it reducing friction. The easier you make a yes, the more likely it happens.
Here's the rewrite. "Totally get it. Would Thursday at seven work, or Sunday afternoon?"
Two clear options cost her one word to answer. If she picks one, she meant it. If she doesn't, you can stop waiting.
[lower voice] Save this for the next time she says she's busy.

## 05 · Dark psychology #20 · "Ghosted after a great date"
Say a girl ghosted you after a great first date, and you keep replaying what you did wrong. [pause] [lower voice] Listen, here's the turn.
Say it went well. You laughed, she hugged you goodbye, and she said she had a good time. Two days later she's gone.
So you decide you must have said something wrong.
[confident] But that's a guess, not a fact.
Psychology calls it avoidance. Some people don't know how to say "I'm not feeling it", so they say nothing. Others just get busy or overwhelmed.
You can't know which, and replaying the date won't tell you.
So send one calm message. "I had a good time. If you're not feeling it, that's okay, just tell me."
Then let it rest. Her silence after that is an answer too.
[lower voice] Send this to the friend who is still replaying his first date.

## 06 · Dark psychology #21 · "Why she texts at night"
Why does she only text me at night? [pause] [lower voice] Listen, it's not what the internet says.
The popular idea is that night texts mean you're a backup. It can be true, but it isn't a rule.
Night is often the only free time she has. Work, classes, and friends are done, and the phone is the first thing she picks up.
Psychology calls it availability, and availability is not the same as priority.
[confident] Here's the move.
Number one. Don't decide yet. One pattern isn't a verdict.
Number two. Ask for something in daylight. "Want to get lunch Saturday?"
Number three. If she says yes, she's making room for you. If it only ever happens at night, you've learned what you're to her.
[lower voice] Save this for the next time her texts only come after ten.
