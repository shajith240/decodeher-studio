# Reference transcripts for the script writer (2026-10-15)
Why: a model writing from scratch uses its default voice, which the owner calls robotic. Method from Youri van Hofwegen ("This AI Writes Better Scripts than 99% of YouTubers"): give the writer real transcripts in the target tone and tell it to match rhythm, word choice and sentence length. Short excerpts, for style study only. Do not copy lines.

## R1 Zoltium, "Your brain fools you" (our target level, 138 wpm, full breakdown in zoltium-story-visuals.md)
"Your brain fools you into telling your plans to others, so you don't actually have to achieve them. Sound weird? Believe it or not, your brain doesn't care about your success. It's wired to keep you alive and save energy. When you tell someone your plans, your brain releases the same chemicals as when you have already achieved them. This makes you feel like the job is done, and you lose the motivation to finish it. And this is how you get fooled, wondering why you can't achieve anything in life."
Copy: complete sentences of 8 to 20 words, "you/your" all the way, one objection line ("Sound weird?"), "so", "when", "and this is how", the last line calls back to line 1. Do not copy: the loose science claims.

## R3 The Josh Speaks, "How to SAVE a DEAD Text Conversation" (YouTube, 269 s, 19k views)
"Let's say you were texting your crush, things were going well, but then suddenly the conversation started to die down. They stopped responding and you didn't know how to revive it. Don't worry, I'm going to explain exactly what you need to do... You start to overthink. What if they're not responding because they don't like me? ... Instead, what I want you to focus on is referencing something you've talked about in the past ... say something like, 'Hey, I haven't seen a good movie since ___. What's another good one I should check out?' ... They're gonna see that message, think about the good time they shared with you, and then actually respond."
Copy: "let's say" opening for a moment, the viewer's own panicking thoughts in quotes, "Don't worry", "Instead", an exact text to send, and the last sentence says WHY it works in plain words.

## R4 Charisma on Command, "If You Run Out Of Things To Say, Play This Simple Game" (YouTube, 258 s, 12M views)
"We've all been there. You're in a conversation, things are humming along nicely, and then out of nowhere the awkward silence shows up. Your mind goes blank, you don't know what to say ... That scenario sucks, so here are five tips ..."
Copy: "We've all been there" then three plain details of the moment, "so" to move on. Sentence length 8 to 18 words, many contractions.

## R5 The Dark Needle, 2026-10-11 (vault, long form)
"There's a girl you've probably met before ... She texts you. She flirts with you. She laughs at the things you say ... So naturally, you're going to ask her out, fully confident that she likes you, but she's busy. Thursday, busy. Friday, can't. Weekend, maybe next week."
Copy: the viewer recognises HER from three small habits, then one concrete run of dates. The short sentences here work because each is a complete action, not a fragment.

## What all five share (the voice to write in)
- Average 10 to 15 words per sentence; fragments only as a single quoted text or a one-word objection.
- "so", "but", "because", "and this is how" between ideas; "Instead," before the fix.
- A named, plain moment ("you're texting your crush") before any explanation.
- The exact text to send is quoted word for word.
- The viewer's own thoughts are said for him ("What if they think I'm boring?").
- Teases that keep him watching: "I'll show you what to text in a second."
