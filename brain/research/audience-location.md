# Audience location: can we steer @thedecodeher toward US / UK / CA / AU?

Researched 2026-10-16. Rule: every number below is a vendor estimate or unverified unless marked "Meta". Meta has published almost nothing on geography for organic Reels, so most of the steering advice is plausible but unconfirmed.

## 1. Can an account choose its organic audience country?

**No. There is no setting for it on organic posts or Reels.**
- Country targeting exists only in paid ads (Meta Ads Manager). Tool vendors state the same: "you can't restrict delivery to a specific country/region" for scheduled organic posts (Khoros help, via search 2026-10-16). Vendor/secondary source, but consistent with everything else found; I found no Meta page contradicting it.
- Reels are a discovery surface. Meta (https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained, fetched 2026-10-16): "the majority of what you see is from accounts you don't follow." Reels are first sourced, then ranked by predicted interest (will this person reshare, watch to the end, like, open the audio page). Signals, roughly in order: the viewer's own activity, the viewer's past interactions with the poster, reel content (audio, visuals, popularity), poster popularity.
- Country is NOT named by Meta as a ranking input. The system matches a reel to viewers who behave like those who already engaged with it. So the effective "audience selector" is **who engages first**. The first few hundred viewers of each reel are a seed audience, and later distribution follows lookalikes of the people who watched, sent and liked it.

What plausibly decides which countries see a reel (secondary sources and logic, not confirmed by Meta):
1. Who engaged with previous reels (the strongest lever, because ranking uses engagement predictions).
2. Language of the voice-over, on-screen text and caption (blogs say language signals relevance; unverified by Meta).
3. Audio track and topic/culture references (US dating slang, US references).
4. Followers: your followers see you first, so a follower base that is 70% India pulls early distribution toward India.
5. Hashtags and location tags: weak and indirect. A Conbersa guide calls geographic targeting of Reels "indirect at best"; Unum claims geotags only help when they match the target region (unverified marketing).
6. Posting time: it changes who is awake and online in the first hour, which changes who seeds the reel. This is real mechanically, but its size is unknown.
7. Instagram users can now steer their OWN feed with "Your Algorithm" topic controls (MediaPost, Search Engine Land via search; US rollout, exact date unclear). This affects topic matching, not geography.

## 2. What credible sources say works for an English page aimed at US/UK/CA/AU

No credible source found a proven method; there is no Meta or Mosseri statement on it. The commonly repeated, low-risk advice (all vendor/blog level, unverified):
- **Language and slang**: native English, US idiom, no Hinglish; burned-in English captions (blog claim: captions lift retention ~38%, unverified, but the mechanism holds).
- **Voice**: a neutral/American-sounding narrator helps viewers decide in 2 s that "this is for me". Our narrator voice is a cloned Chatterbox voice; test an American accent against the current one.
- **Topics and references**: US dating culture (texting, apps, ghosting, "situationship", "red flags"), US cities in examples; avoid India-specific cultural references.
- **Hashtags / location tags**: a few niche English tags; a US city tag on some reels is a cheap test but expect little. Do not use geography-spam tags.
- **Collaboration**: collab posts and comments on US dating/relationship creators put you in front of their audience. Sources: general creator advice; unverified for effect size.
- **Posting time**: see section 3.
- **Engagement seeding**: reply to and follow US accounts in the niche; pods and bought engagement are excluded (they poison the seed audience and break the lookalike logic).

## 3. Posting times for a US audience, in IST

Data: Sprout Social, published 2026-03-31 (fetched 2026-10-16): ~2 billion engagements, ~307,000 profiles, 2025-11-27 to 2026-02-27, all in the viewer's LOCAL time. Best for Instagram overall: Tuesday 1-7 pm, Wednesday 12-9 pm (also Monday 2-4 pm, Thursday 12-2 pm). Weekends worst. No separate Reels times. Vendor estimate, all content types combined.
Other vendor numbers (disagree, so low weight): SocialPilot (~250k Reels, 2025): Mon-Thu 8 am-12 pm; Later (2024 data, 6M posts): Monday, 12-6 am, best hour 5 am; RivalIQ via a secondary blog: Saturday 10 pm-7 am. Buffer's own page (2020) gives no fixed times and says to use your own Analytics.

Our current slots (08:30, 21:00 IST, plus the new third):
- 08:30 IST = 23:00 ET / 20:00 PT the previous evening (EDT): a decent US evening slot (14:00 in Sydney; late night in the UK).
- 21:00 IST = 11:30 ET / 08:30 PT: US late morning, fine.
- Suggested third slot for the test: 22:30-23:30 IST (US lunch to early afternoon on the east coast, within Sprout's Tue/Wed window) or 04:30-05:30 IST (US evening).

Caveat: Reels can keep being distributed for days, so exact posting hour matters less than the first-hour response by the right people.

## 4. Trade-offs for a men's dating-psychology niche

Size (vendor estimates, mid-2026; Meta does not publish country totals):
- NapoleonCat (from Meta ad-reach tools, estimate, June 2026): India ~553M Instagram users, 69.5% men; US ~192M, 55% women.
- DataReportal figures via a secondary blog: India ~480.6M vs US ~181.8M. Methods differ; do not mix.
- Implication: India has the largest male Instagram audience in the world by far, and Reels distribution to Indian men is easy. The US has fewer men on Instagram, but they are the more valuable audience.
Monetization (all vendor estimates, no Meta numbers):
- US Instagram ad CPM estimates roughly $5-15; India Reels roughly Rs 45-140 per 1,000 impressions (elev8or.io, Oct 2026; Instagram publishes no official CPM). YouTube CPM (a proxy only): ~$32.75 US vs ~$0.70 India (2025-26 compilation).
- Brand deals and course/affiliate offers in dating (coaching, apps, grooming, books) pay more with US/UK/CA/AU audiences; Indian sponsorships exist but at much lower rates (anecdotal Rs 500-3,000 per sponsored reel for nano creators).
Follower quality and risks of a mixed audience:
- A mixed audience is not harmful per se. The risk is that early engagers decide the seed audience: if Indian viewers engage most, the system sends more reels to Indian viewers, and US-targeted language gets weaker feedback. This is a loop, so shifting it takes consistent signals over weeks.
- Dating content with explicit manipulation framing can be flagged under recommendation guidelines in any country; keep language practical and non-abusive.
- Some topics (e.g. "texting a woman who ghosted you", American app culture) have lower relevance for part of the Indian audience, which lowers watch time and hurts distribution. Watch time is the top signal, so a niche-fit audience in any country beats a mismatched one.
- Do not buy followers or use pods to shift location: this ruins retention data and may violate policy.

How to read audience country (Meta, https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/api-reference/instagram-user/insights):
- Metric `follower_demographics`: "demographic characteristics of followers, including countries, cities and gender distribution", breakdowns age, city, country, gender. "Not returned if the IG User has less than 100 followers." (Meta, fetched 2026-10-16.) We have 6 followers, so no country data yet.
- In the app: Professional dashboard > Insights > Total followers > Top locations (Later's docs: top 5 countries and cities as % of followers; requires a Business/Creator account). Reel-level: open the reel > Insights; viewer country by reel is not shown at small scale in some accounts, so use account-level Reach/Views breakdown ("Followers vs Non-followers") and follower location.
- Until 100 followers, infer from comments, profile visits and who follows (usernames, language).

## 5. Low-risk experiment plan (no blanket switch)

Goal: raise the share of US/UK/CA/AU viewers and followers without breaking watch time. Primary decision rule: do not accept a change that lowers average watch time or 3-second retention by more than 15% relative.
Phase 0 (now to 100 followers, ~2 weeks): change nothing about content. Log reach, 3 s skip, avg watch time, sends, follows per reel. Collect profile-visit sources by hand: after each batch, note the geography of commenters/followers.
Phase 1 (weeks 1-2 after 100 followers): read Top locations. Record baseline split (e.g. % India, % US, UK, CA, AU). Keep posting both current slots.
Phase 2 (weeks 3-4): A/B on time. Move 1 of 3 daily reels per day to a US-evening/lunch slot (22:30-23:30 IST or 04:30-05:30 IST) and keep the other two at current times. Compare follower country mix and watch time by slot (small sample, directional only).
Phase 3 (weeks 5-6): A/B on language. For half the reels, US-flavoured script (US texting slang, apps, "situationship") and an American narrator voice; other half unchanged. Keep topics matched so the only variable is language.
Phase 4 (ongoing): weekly 10 minutes of real engagement: comment on 10 US/UK dating or relationship creator reels, answer all comments, and collab/guest where possible. Add one US city location tag on a few reels as a free test.
Measure: follower Top locations (weekly), non-follower reach share, avg watch time, 3 s skip, sends per reach, follows per 1,000 views, comments by language. A change counts as a win only if US-group share rises for 2 consecutive weeks AND watch time does not fall.
Stop conditions: watch time or reach falls more than 15% after a change (revert), or the Indian audience share does not move after 6 weeks (then accept a mixed audience and tailor monetization: affiliate/offers sold globally, treat the US as upside).
Re-check this note monthly; Meta behaviour changes.
