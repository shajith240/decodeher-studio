## 1. The five things that matter most

1. **One fixed format, reused 72 times out of 72.** The cover is a big photo of a woman or couple with a black
   gradient over the bottom 45%. The headline is a 6–17-word sentence in heavy uppercase sans, white with 1–3 keywords
   in **yellow (#FEC619)**. Every inner slide is the same "tweet card": a flat dark navy (#001523) background, an
   avatar, a name and handle, and plain left-aligned white text. He never designs anything new. The cover alone does
   the work of the post.
2. **Each cover headline makes one of three promises:** a number with a type ("6 types of girls you should stay away
   from"), a shocking claim ("Marrying a woman over 30 is like buying a newspaper in the evening"), or a body fact
   ("This is what happens to a woman's body if she has sex every single day"). The headline is a full sentence, so it
   can be read in about 2 seconds.
3. **The image is the scroll-stopper, and it is always a woman's face or body, or a couple at an intimate moment.**
   He mixes photoreal AI women, film and TV stills (Titanic, Wednesday, WandaVision, House of the Dragon, Fifty Shades),
   and soft anime or pencil illustrations. The mood is warm, low-key and cinematic.
4. **The top concepts are "useful to keep" or "worth arguing about."** Body and sex science, and screening (red flags,
   green flags, types to avoid), get the most likes. Hot takes about women (age 30+, looks, race) get the most comments,
   with comments up to 118% of likes. Texting scripts come in third. Decoding her behaviour, which is our niche, is his
   *weakest* type (median 510 likes). That is a gap we can own if we package it his way.
5. **The last slide sells his book and asks one comment question** ("How many of these effects did you already know
   about?" / "Comment VALUE if you believe…"). Saves come from numbered, reference-style lists: 6 to 10 items, one item
   per slide, each with a bold claim line and a 2–4-line explanation.

## 3. Slide 1: the cover formula

**Layout:** 4:5 portrait (1080×1350 or 1440×1800). The photo fills the top 55–60%, and a gradient blends it to pure
black (#000) across the lower half. The headline is centred in the bottom 40%. There is a small "SWIPE →" pill in the
bottom-right corner on about half the posts. No logo, no border, no number badge.

**Type:** a geometric heavy sans (Montserrat ExtraBold or Black), ALL CAPS, centred, 3–5 lines, very tight leading, and
large: the cap height is about 5% of the slide height. White text with **1–3 yellow (#FEC619) keywords**: the topic noun
and the payoff, e.g. "**MEN** … **CAREFUL** … **CHOOSING A GIRL**". Spicy words are censored (S*X, FU*KING) to get past
the filters and to add a little charge.

**Word count:** 5–17 words, typically 9–13. It is a full sentence, not a label.

**Headline patterns, with his real covers:**
| Pattern | Example (likes) |
|---|---|
| "This is what happens to ___ if ___" | "This is what happens to a woman's body if she has sex every single day" (46.6k) |
| "[Imperative] her if she has these N ___ (why men learn too late)" | "F*cking marry her if she has these 10 green flags (why men learn too late)" (4.6k) |
| Metaphor shock: "X is like Y" | "Marrying a woman over 30 is like buying a newspaper in the evening… the news is already old" (4.5k / 844 comments) |
| "[Group] must be very careful in ___" | "Single men must be very careful in choosing a girl" (4.1k) |

**Why it stops the scroll:** a face, usually a woman looking into or just past the lens, catches the eye first. The
yellow words give the topic in a single glance. The full sentence opens a curiosity gap ("what happens?", "which 10?",
"what's the real reason?"), and only swiping closes it. The number promises a quick, finite read.

## 4. The image recipe

| Source | Share (approx., from the 58 covers seen) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Photoreal AI women or couples (glamour, bedroom, selfie, city at dusk) | ~45% | Low-cut tops and soft window light. Some look obviously generated. |
| Film and TV stills with real actors (Titanic, Wednesday, WandaVision, House of the Dragon, Fifty Shades, Normal People-style) | ~30% | Uses real faces and copyrighted stills. **We must not copy this** (our rule: no real or famous faces). |
| Illustration (soft anime couples, flat pop illustration, pencil sketch, Avatar fan art) | ~25% | He uses this for the more explicit sex topics. Illustration keeps them feed-safe. |

- **Subject:** about 80% feature a woman, either alone with direct eye contact or in an intimate couple moment (a kiss,
  an embrace, a whisper). Men appear alone only on "how to text like a bad boy" type covers.
- **Grade:** warm, low-key and filmic (amber or tungsten skin, dark backgrounds). The picture blends into black at the
  bottom, so the page reads as "one dark brand" even though the images come from everywhere.
- **Inner slides have no images at all.** They are text only. All the visual effort goes into the cover.
- **Image and text never overlap the face.** The face sits in the top half, and the headline stays on black.

**Our version** (fits our rules: no real or famous faces, no AI covers made with Codex unless the owner asks, and the
owner rejected generated covers): use stills from our own approved pools, `library/hook-pool/ai/` (adult, no nudity,
not famous) and the colour "dark psychology" Pinterest clips. Pick a frame with a face looking near the lens. Apply our
cinematic grade and blend to black. The emotional temperature should be tension (a glance, a phone glow, a half-turned
face), not cleavage.

- 6 slides: 27 posts
- 7 slides: 14
- 8 slides: 9
- 9 slides: 7
- 10 slides: 6

**His flow:**
1. **Cover:** the promise.
2. **Slides 2 to N−1:** one numbered point per slide (sometimes two on 6-slide posts). Each point gets a short claim
   line (often bold, or "1. SILENT TREATMENT" in caps) and a 2–5-line explanation in plain words, 25–60 words per slide.
   The texting posts use ❌ "what most men send" / ✅ "what to send instead": 2 lines per slide, about 15 words.
3. **Last slide:** either the final point with a one-line moral ("Men who don't screen carefully pay for the rest of
   their lives."), or a book plug with a question ("📘 Want to understand women… Get my book… How many of these effects
   did you already know about?"), or a red product mock-up ("Click the link in the bio to unlock it").

**The tweet card** (every inner slide):
- background: flat dark navy #001523
- top-left: a round avatar (a bronze, muscular statue torso), the bold name "Manly Pinnacle" and a grey @handle
- body: system sans (Roboto or Helvetica-like) at about 28–32 px on 1080, left-aligned, generous line gaps, no images

## 6. Captions, hashtags, cadence

- **Captions:** median 58 words. Line 1 restates the headline as a softer, "reasonable" thesis ("Choosing a partner

- **Hashtags:** about 2.6 per post. The most used are #relationships (30 posts), #dating, #datingadvice, #selfrespect,
  #communication and #relationshipadvice. This matches our 3-hashtag rule.
- **Cadence:** about 1 post a day through September, rising to **3–4 a day from 15 October**. He churns posts because
  each one takes almost no effort to make (one image plus text cards).
- **Calls to action seen:** "Save this", "Share this with a man you care about", "Comment VALUE / SURVIVAL if you believe…",
  "How many of these did you already know?", "Kings, what's your take?", "Click the link in the bio to unlock it".

## 7. Why people save and share (the mechanisms)

1. **Reference value:** numbered lists of facts or flags ("10 green flags", "5 tests") that a man will want to check
   again against a real woman later. This is the main reason people save.
2. **Copy-paste value:** texting posts give exact lines (❌/✅). People save them to use the lines.
3. **Repeatable fact:** body-science posts give a man something surprising to tell a friend or partner. This drives
   sends.
4. **Identity and agreement:** "a man is allowed to have standards" gets sent to friends as a way of saying "this is
   us".
5. **Curiosity gap plus a finite number:** "these 10", so people swipe to the end, and he counts every swipe through
   as a completed read.
6. **Controversy:** comments and quote-arguments boost reach, but this brings the wrong audience and doesn't fit our
   ethics.

## 9. Plugging this into `~/.claude/skills/ultimate-carousel-maker`

The skill was built for a different look: a giant one-word condensed title, "no numbers or badges", Codex-generated
covers, rotating palettes and illustrated "worlds". His winning formula contradicts several of those defaults. To use
the skill for DecodeHer:

1. **Add a new world: `decode-card`** (`references/style-worlds.md` §3 + `design/worlds.json` + a theme in
   `src/render/themes.js`):
   - **Cover:** a full-bleed graded still in the top 60%, blended to black, and a centred uppercase heavy-sans sentence
     headline (6–13 words) with 1–3 accent words. This overrides the "giant one-word title" and "no numbers" rules in
     §1 for this world. Numbers are the hook here.
   - **Inner slides:** our version of the tweet card. Flat near-black (or our deep tone), the bronze-bust avatar plus
     "DecodeHer" and @thedecodeher, left-aligned body text, and a numbered bold claim line. No images. Keep the skill's
     continuity rule: a thin accent line and the same accent colour as the cover's keywords.
2. **Image source:** turn off Codex cover generation for this brand (our rule: Codex only when the owner explicitly
   asks; generated covers were rejected). Feed the poster step a still picked from `library/hook-pool/ai/` or our
   Pinterest clip library, using the same CLIP picker and banned-text check as `clippick.py`, plus our grade.
3. **Copy limits in `src/checks.js` / `config.json`:**
   - allow cover headlines of up to about 70 characters, with 1–3 highlights instead of 1
   - inner slides: keep ≤ 45 words, and add a `xcheck` slide kind for ❌/✅ text pairs (2 lines, ≤ 20 words)
   - `carousel.minSlides` 6, `maxSlides` 10
   - `caption.hashtagsMax` 3
4. **Topic prompt (`src/prompts/topic.md`):** rank topic types by the table in §2. Weight screening, decode lists,
   ❌/✅ texting and "what happens in her brain" science. Ban rage-bait (age, race, body count).
5. **CTA:** the skill bans "comment X" as engagement bait. Keep that ban, and use our natural closing lines instead
   (SEND vs SAVE experiment, `brain/growth/tactics/send-or-save-closing-line.md`): "Save this for the next time she ___"
   or "Send this to the friend who's overthinking her text." Add one honest question.
6. **Show before you ship:** render one proof carousel in `decode-card` and show the owner the sheet before anything
   gets posted (house rule).
