## 2. Hook patterns (first 1-3 s)

Patterns below come from creator-marketing sources (weak evidence). Example lines are **illustrative drafts I wrote, not lines copied from real reels**.

1. Contrarian claim: "Being nice is why she's not texting back." (Source: https://www.inro.social/blog/instagram-reels-3-second-hook-leads, generic guidance on contrarian hooks)
2. Specific number: "3 texts that make women chase you." (Specificity tactic, same source)
3. Direct question: "Why do you get friendzoned every time?"
4. Pattern interrupt on-screen text: "Stop doing this on the first date." (On-screen text in first 3 s, source above)
5. Open loop: "The reason she pulled away isn't what you think. Wait for it."
6. Named concept: "This is called push-pull, and it's why she's confused." (Concept-naming, framed as research-backed in https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-make-good-instagram-reels)
7. Red-flag callout: "Red flag: she only texts when she's bored."
8. Relatable scenario: "You just sent a long text and got one word back."
9. Bold claim about the viewer: "You're smarter than this, so why do you keep doing it?"
10. Visual shock or sound hit: no text, a loud cut on frame 1 (generic pattern-interrupt advice, source above).

Note: the "50% drop-off in 3 seconds" and "60% hold rate" figures circulate in marketing posts. **Unverified**, not used here.
