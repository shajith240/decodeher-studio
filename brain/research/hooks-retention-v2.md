## 1. Diagnosis: why our scripts lose people
1. **The hook is a scene-setter with a happy feeling and no loss.** p1: "It's one in the morning and she's sending you memes, and you're in bed laughing like an idiot." It runs 19 words, about 8 s at our 2.3 words/s. A good moment for him, so nothing is at risk. The real hook ("Crazy. But she had time at one a.m., right?") sits at about 13 s, after the swipe.
2. **Our own rule 2 was too vague.** "Drop him into the moment" gives a setting, not tension. A hook needs a claim, a threat or an unanswered question in the first 4-5 words.
3. **Hooks are long.** w1-07 "Everyone says look away so she doesn't think you're creepy, but get this, looking away is what kills it." has 21 words before the point. w1-03 "this one's for you" and w1-07 "get this" are filler. w1-06 "POV: you're an hour into…" is a label, with no stakes.
4. **Payoff and "why" are abstract and late.** p1: "you're already giving her the fun part for free" lands at about 20 s. The picture in the viewer's head is unclear. Compare w1-05 "you're basically furniture", which is concrete and works.
5. **No re-hook.** Lines run on as "and… so… because". Nothing new is promised between second 4 and the end, so a viewer who survives the hook has no question left.
6. **Every reel ends in the same shape** (spoken CTA after a flat tip). The viewer gets no rewatch trigger and no reason to comment. "I've been that guy" is generic. A named detail is better.
7. **What works already:** w1-04 hook states the cost ("You're missing girls who already like you…"). w1-05 "That wasn't luck." is a real beat. w1-02 opens with a confession. Keep those moves.
8. Zoltium holds on picture rhythm (a new image every ~1.5 s), not on faster speech (see zoltium-story-visuals.md). Our words problem is the hook and the missing mid-reel question. The visual problem is separate.

## 2. The hook
**First 1.5 s.** Voice gives 3-6 words that state one of: a verdict, a cost, a quoted line, or a question the viewer can't answer. The screen shows the same thing: the key words (3-5, big) plus a phone bubble or a face reaction, never an empty wide shot. The viewer must know the topic AND feel a stake before second 2. Full hook ≤ 12 words / ≤ 4 s [P: 7-12 words] .

**Rules.** (a) Claim first, scene second. (b) Specific beats general ("1 a.m.", "three weeks", "'this week is crazy'"). (c) Don't resolve the claim in the hook; promise the reason. (d) Name the cost to HIM, not a fact about her. (e) No intro, no "did you know", no "POV", no "here's why". (f) What we say about her is behaviour ("she's keeping you as entertainment"), never insult. (g) No lie: the reel must pay what the hook promised.

**12 formulas** (template, then 3 examples)
1. **Verdict-first.** "[Behaviour] means [uncomfortable read]." — "A girl who texts at 1 a.m. but can't meet is keeping you as entertainment." / "If she replies in four hours every time, she's not busy." / "A girl who 'needs space' on Friday but memes you Saturday has already decided."
2. **Quoted line.** "She said '[line].' Here's what it meant." — "She said 'this week is crazy.' It wasn't." / "'Let's just be friends' means one thing, and it isn't kindness." / "'I'm bad at texting' is a sentence, not a personality."
3. **Stop / cost.** "Stop [habit]. It's costing you [thing]." — "Stop answering her at 1 a.m. It's costing you the date." / "Stop texting good morning. She's getting used to you." / "Stop telling her how you feel on week two."
4. **If-she-does-X.** "If a girl [does X], [do/means Y]." — "If a girl looks twice, she's already given you a signal." / "If she says 'we should hang out sometime', ask for a day." / "If she laughs at nothing near you, it isn't nothing."
5. **Number + consequence.** "[Number] [time/count], [bad result]." — "Three weeks of memes. Zero dates." / "Forty good-morning texts, and she forgot my name." / "One question on a first date, and she checked her phone."
6. **Confession.** "I [lost thing] because I [mistake]." — "I lost a girl over one text, sent at 1 a.m." / "I told a girl I loved her on day twelve. She vanished by day fourteen." / "I replied in 30 seconds for a month. She stopped replying at all."
7. **Contrarian.** "[Common advice] is [wrong in a specific way]." — "'Just be yourself' got me zero dates." / "Playing it cool isn't why she lost interest. Boredom is." / "Being nice isn't your problem. Being easy to predict is."
8. **Named gap.** "There's a reason [odd behaviour], and it's [teaser]." — "There's a reason she goes cold right after you're nice." / "There's a reason the second date feels worse." / "There's a reason she's warm online and cold in person."
9. **Direct call-out / "you".** "If you [exact situation], [stake]." — "If you've texted 'drinks Friday?' and got 'crazy week', watch this." / "If she hasn't replied since 9 p.m., don't send a second text." / "If you freeze when she walks up, you're doing one thing wrong."
10. **Scene + stake.** "[Time, detail]. [What's about to go wrong]." — "1 a.m. She sends a meme. Friday, she's 'busy'." / "She looked at you three times. You looked at your drink." / "You typed 'hey' four times and deleted it four times."
11. **Question he can't answer.** "Why does [she] [do odd thing] when [contradiction]?" — "Why is she never free, but always online?" / "Why do the good guys get the 'you're like a brother' text?" / "Why did she go quiet right after the best date?"
12. **Dare / forbidden.** "Don't [common act] until you hear this." — "Don't reply to her next meme until you hear this." / "Don't ask her out by text until you read this." / "Never say 'what do you want to do' on a first date."

## 4. Script skeleton (35 s, ~85-95 words at our 2.3 w/s)
| s | beat | job |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1.5 | HOOK | formula 1/2/3/6, ≤12 words, loop A opens ("why?") |
| 1.5-5 | STAKES | cost to him in one line, plus the exact detail ("three weeks, zero dates") |
| 5-13 | STORY / EXAMPLE | one night, two quoted lines (her words, his words), concrete objects |
| ~12 | RE-HOOK | "But look at the timing." / "Here's what she didn't say." Loop B opens |
| 13-22 | TWIST / REVEAL | the contradiction (1 a.m. vs "crazy week") |
| 22-27 | THE WHY | one plain sentence, one picture ("you're free entertainment", "furniture") |
| 27-32 | THE MOVE | one thing to say or stop doing, quoted |
| 32-35 | CTA + LOOP | CTA with "follow for more", last words flow into line 1 |

Re-hooks: every 4-6 s change something (new image per phrase, a "but" or "so", a quoted line). The 5-7 s rule is a heuristic [P]; the only sourced cadence is a scene change every 2-4 s. Chain with **but / so / because** ("She texts at 1 a.m. BUT says she's busy SO you wait BECAUSE…"), never "and then".
**Rewatch:** the last line completes the first line's grammar, or answers its question only after a second look ("…and that's why Friday never happens."). **Comment:** end on a side-choice ("Been the meme guy? Tell me how long.") that's easy to answer in four words.

## 5. Clarity and storyteller rules
- **Words:** 8-12 per line, ≤2 clauses, one idea per reel. On-screen: 3-5 words per caption group (Kallaway [P]), 6 words per line max. Reading load ≤ 15 characters/s (Netflix 20 cps is a ceiling for adults).
- **Level:** aim ≤ grade 5-6 [P: vendor claim of 2x views, unverified]. Test: would a 15-year-old say it aloud? One- and two-syllable words; swap "attention" for "interest" only if it sounds natural.
- **Concrete nouns over abstractions:** "phone", "1 a.m.", "Friday", "drinks". No "dynamic", "validation", "investment".
- **Show-don't-tell:** one quoted exchange beats three sentences of explanation.
- **Storyteller voice:** second person ("you"), present tense for the scene, past tense only for "I" confessions. Contractions always. One rhetorical question aimed at his objection ("Busy? At 1 a.m.?"). Spoken rhythm: a short line after a long one. First person once ("I did this").
- **Cut:** setup before the claim, adverbs, "very/really/literally", lists of three fragments, "It's not X, it's Y", "Here's the thing", moral closers, anything he has to re-read in his head.
- **Fair to her:** state her view in her words so the decode feels fair ("She's not lying, she's just not choosing you yet").

## 6. Scorecard (grade before voicing; pass ≥ 8/10)
1. The first 5 words state a claim, cost, quote or question. (yes/no)
2. Hook ≤ 12 words and ≤ 4 s. (yes/no)
3. Hook shows a stake to HIM within 2 s. (0-1)
4. Hook names a concrete detail (time, number, quote). (yes/no)
5. A reason to keep watching is promised, not given. (yes/no)
6. A second open question or "but" turn appears at 10-14 s. (yes/no)
7. Whole script has one idea and one example; the "why" is a single sentence with a picture. (0-2)
8. Average line ≤ 11 words, no stacked clauses, ≥ 3 contractions. (yes/no)
9. One move he can copy in a quoted line. (yes/no)
10. Last line loops to the first or invites a 4-word comment, and says "follow for more". (0-2)
