# Value density in short form (2026-10-15)
Why: owner says our reels are thin ("why would they follow?"). Our data: 0 saves, 0 sends, 0 comments on 376 reach, avg watch ~10 s ([[growth/growth-plan-2026-10-15]]).
Honesty note: web search gave only marketing blogs (no platform data) and nothing usable on Charisma on Command shorts. The findings below rest mainly on our own transcripts (reference-transcripts.md, zoltium-story-visuals.md, competitors-2026-10.md) and are marked [V] checked in a file, [W] marketing-blog claim, [I] my inference.

## What a viewer walks away with (the pattern in the best creators)
1. **Zoltium R1** [V]: the claim, then the MECHANISM in plain words ("your brain is wired to save energy... releases the same chemicals"), then the cost ("you lose the motivation"), then a callback to line 1. Value = one named reason, 138 wpm, no story padding. Weak point: loose science, so we copy the structure, not the claims.
2. **The Dark Needle** [V]: names the pattern first ("whoever chases, loses"), then walks the cause step by step with a labelled diagram. Value = a phrase he can repeat. Their long form hides the answer; a 40 s reel can't.
3. **The Josh Speaks R3** [V]: "Don't worry, I'm going to explain exactly what you need to do", the viewer's panic thought in quotes, then an EXACT text with a blank to fill, then one sentence on why it works. Value = copy-paste move.
4. **Charisma on Command R4** [V]: "We've all been there" plus three concrete details, then a named, repeatable tool (a game). Value = a tool with a name.
5. **Blaine, Beyond Attraction** [V, competitors-2026-10 l.199]: "mistake, then cost" ("most men send this one") = the guy's own instinct is called out as the error.
6. **Indian men's pages / others** [I]: not checked in detail. Most lean on a bold verdict plus a promise; the ones that grow add an exact line to say.

## The value ladder (what each reel should contain, in order)
1. Her behaviour + what it really means (first 2 sentences, under 6 s).
2. The mechanism: WHY she does it, in one plain sentence with "because". This is the "insider knowledge" feeling: he learns something he could not guess.
3. The mistake: what his instinct does, and the exact way it backfires ("so she has nothing to answer").
4. Two concrete moves, word for word. Two is the minimum that feels like a toolkit; one feels like a tip.
5. One repeatable line ("a chat only keeps going when someone asks a question"). This is what he tells his friend.
6. A prompt that matches the content (save for exact words, send for "your friend who...", comment for her text).
7. A closing line unique to this reel.

## Speed to value
- Mechanism by ~8 s, first move by ~20 s [V: our posts lose people by 12-15 s, growth plan cause 1]. A friend-story is at most ONE sentence; it sets up the mistake, it is not the content.
- Longer is fine only if second 15-35 each add a new thing. Our avg watch is 10 s, so the retention fix is front-loading the "why", not padding the end.

## What makes people SAVE vs SEND
- SAVE: exact words to copy, a rule he'll need later, a checklist of signs (three signs, not one) [W, several blogs: educational how-tos earn saves].
- SEND: a verdict about something a specific girl did, aimed at "your friend who..." [W, Mosseri/SocialPilot via the growth plan: sends per reach is the key non-follower signal]. Relatable content earns sends, useful content earns saves; we want both in one reel.
- Caution [W]: one source says carousels beat reels for saves (Metricool, unverified). Keep a carousel repost in mind for the best scripts.

## Insider-knowledge feel (ESL audience)
- Say the principle as a rule in grade-5 words ("people laugh more around someone they like"), not a term. Name a principle only if the name is simple.
- Use "usually", "often", "most" to stay accurate. Avoid "always", "never", "means".
- Respect: the move is honest and low-pressure. No tactics that hide intent, no "make her chase".

## Rules we take from this
Mechanism with "because" in every script; the mistake line; two moves in quotes; one repeatable line; friend-story at most one sentence; closing line is the insight, not a slogan. Test lives in [[rules/spoken-script-rules]].
