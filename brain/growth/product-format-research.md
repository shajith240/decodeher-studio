# Product format research: what to sell, and in what form (2026-10-19)

Owner's worry: "Who reads a 73-page PDF? Our audience isn't nerds."
Short answer: **he is right about the framing, wrong that the content is wrong.** Almost nobody reads a how-to PDF cover to cover, and nobody needs to. What he buys is a lookup: "she texted X, what do I send?" So sell it as a lookup tool (find your situation, copy the reply), and stop selling it as a 73-page book.

Labels: **[DATA]** = a measured number from a named source. **[CLAIM]** = seller or vendor statement, unaudited. **[OPINION]** = my judgement. Searches were run 2026-10-19. Honest caveat: I found **no** audited data for low-ticket dating products, sales numbers, or a head-to-head "cheat sheet vs ebook" test. Where evidence is thin, I say so.

What this means for us:
- **[DATA]** Even people who bought a *novel-length book for reading* finish non-fiction well under half the time. Our reader is a 20-year-old who found us on a Reel. Assume he reads a few pages, not 73.
- **[OPINION]** A 73-page PDF is not a failure if he opens it at the moment he needs one page. The real risk is that he *feels* he bought homework. That feeling, not the usage, causes refunds and bad word of mouth.
- **[OPINION]** "Completion" is the wrong metric for a reference product. The right ones are: does he find his situation in under 30 seconds, and does he send the reply.

## 3. Reference/lookup versus read-cover-to-cover

**What I could not find:** any controlled comparison. Searches for "cheat sheet vs ebook" sales data returned only sales pages and unsourced blog posts. Be straight with the owner: this section is **opinion backed by indirect evidence.**

Indirect evidence:
- **[DATA]** Non-fiction completion is below 40% even when people bought it to read (section 1). Linear reading is the exception.
- **[DATA, platform-displayed]** The one texting listing with a sales count that I found is built on "21 plug-and-play text templates for any scenario" (Gumroad).
- **[DATA]** The cheapest competitors sell "templates organised by situation" at $7-9. The product people can picture using in the moment is already the norm.
- **[OPINION]** Our traffic is short-form video viewers who want an answer in seconds. A Reel promises one answer to one situation. The matching product is "your situation, one answer." A situation finder keeps the promise the Reel made.
- **[OPINION]** Our product is already structured as a reference (25 situations, 2 pages each, never-send list, cheat sheet). The format is fine. The packaging and the promise are the problem.

## 4. Packaging options, ranked

Scoring: build effort for a solo operator, fit to this audience, refund risk, and whether it needs new tooling.

### Rank 1 (do now, 1 day, no new infra): re-frame the PDF as a lookup, and add a 5-minute start
- Lead with the job: **"She texted. Find your situation in 10 seconds. Copy the reply."**
- Add a **one-page "Start here"** at the front: 3 steps (find her text in the index, read what it usually means, copy the reply) plus "if you only read one page, read this."
- Add a **clickable index** on page 2 (PDF internal links) and PDF bookmarks, grouped by what she did ("she went quiet", "she's dry", "she cancelled").
- The Reply Bank and a **plain .txt file for the Notes app** become the headline, not a bonus: he can copy-paste without opening the PDF. (Bonuses already planned in brain/growth/pricing-offer.md; just reorder their prominence.)
- Lock-screen cheat sheets (already planned) are the "5-second" product.
- Effort: low. Refund risk: lowest, because it changes expectations, not the product.

### Rank 2 (build next, 2-4 days): mobile web "situation finder" behind the purchase
- A static page on Cloudflare: tap "She left me on read" and see the read and the reply with a copy button. 25 situations plus the Signals field guide entries, searchable by plain words.
- Gate it with a Polar **license key**. Polar supports license keys (brandable prefix, activation limits, expiry) and **file downloads up to 10 GB**, automatically on purchase: https://polar.sh/docs/features/benefits/license-keys , https://polar.sh/features/benefits . The page checks the key in a Worker with Polar's validate endpoint (public, no secret needed, but confirm in docs when building).
- **Do not make this a replacement for the PDF.** Ship both: PDF as the file download, app as the "open on your phone" link. Some buyers want a file they own.
- Risks: a shared key is easy to leak (mitigate with activation limit, e.g. 3 devices); more support questions; Cloudflare/Polar are already in place, so cost is time, not money.
- **[OPINION]** This is the strongest long-term form because it matches how he will actually use it (phone, one-handed, mid-conversation), and it gives us the product-update story ("new situations added weekly") that justifies $27 and gives buyers a reason to return.

### Rank 3 (later, only after 20+ buyers): AI "paste her text" decoder
- Demand is proven by Rizz/Yazcam (section 2), but they are funded and in the app stores, and subscriptions are what they sell.
- **Cost:** with Claude Haiku 4.5 at $1 per million input and $5 per million output tokens (https://pricepertoken.com/pricing-page/model/anthropic-claude-haiku-4.5 ; confirm against Anthropic's pricing page; one blog claims a cheaper newer Haiku, unverified), a decode of ~500 tokens in and ~300 out is roughly **$0.002** (a fifth of a cent). Even 100 decodes per buyer is about $0.20. Cost is not the blocker. **Abuse is**: someone scripts the endpoint. Needs per-key rate limit (license key helps) and a monthly cap.
- **Ethics:** it would receive another person's private messages. Rules if built: do not store texts, say so on the page, never ask him to paste her *identifying* details, refuse "how do I make her insecure/dependent" (matches CLAUDE.md ethics), and be honest that it guesses ("often means"), not mind-reads. Wrong reads cause harm (he chases a "no").
- Effort: medium-high, plus ongoing quality and safety review. Not for a solo, faceless page with no reviews right now.
- **[OPINION]** Offer it later as the upsell ("Decode Her Live") once the finder exists and people ask for it.

### Rank 4: audio version
- Cheap to make with our Fish voice (note: Fish commercial terms are flagged unclear in memory, voice-and-assets-licensing.md; resolve before selling audio).
- No evidence it sells in this niche. Likely a nice-to-have bonus, not a reason to buy. Skip until asked.

### Not recommended now
- Short video course: high build time, **[CLAIM]** self-paced course completion 5-15%, and our whole brand is faceless Reels, which already give away video for free.
- Private community: empty-room problem at ~35 followers; moderation load; refund fights.
- Notion template: wrong audience.
- Email course: fine as a *free* lead magnet (our /free page is that), not a paid product.

## 5. Refund and satisfaction risk, realistic for a solo, faceless seller with no reviews

- **[CLAIM, unsourced]** Digital product refund rates are quoted at ~1-2% for art and ~5-8% for marketing courses (https://profitable.app/tools/gumroad-revenue-calculator). Nothing specific for ebooks. Plan on **3-8%** as a working assumption, not a fact.
- **[DATA]** On Gumroad, fees are not returned on refund (https://gumroad.com/help/article/47-how-to-refund-a-customer). Check Polar's current refund and fee terms before launch; I did not verify them.
- Biggest driver of refunds for a no-reviews seller is **expectation mismatch**: "I thought it was a quick trick" or "I got a book." The fix is honest, specific promises on the page, not a weaker product.

| Format | Refund / satisfaction risk | Why |
|---|---|---|
| PDF sold as "73-page playbook" (today) | Medium | Feels like homework; "I didn't read it" is a reason to refund |
| PDF sold as a lookup with Start-here and index | Low-medium | He gets an answer in the first minute |
| Copy-paste Reply Bank / .txt | Low | Instant, visible value |
| Mobile finder | Low-medium | Friction if key login fails; support load |
| AI decoder | Medium-high | Wrong reads, safety complaints, abuse, privacy questions |
| Course / community | High | Time commitment and no results quickly |

Solo-operator rules:
- Keep the 30-day no-questions refund (already planned). With ~$17-27 prices, a refund costs less than a dispute or a chargeback.
- Show the proof we do have: a **free sample** (/free is the 12 never-send texts), real page screenshots, and a "what's inside" with the true counts. No fake reviews, no fake counters (FTC deceptive pricing rule is already noted in pricing-offer.md).
- Ask the first 20 buyers one question by email ("what did you use it for first?") and quote only replies we have permission to quote.

## Ranked recommendation

1. **Keep the product, change the promise (now).** Sell "Find your exact situation in seconds, copy the reply," not "73 pages." Add the Start-here page, clickable index and bookmarks, and make the copy-paste Reply Bank + Notes .txt the first thing he sees.
2. **Build the mobile situation finder (next, 2-4 days)** and deliver it with a Polar license key alongside the PDF. This is the form that actually fits his phone-in-hand moment, and it backs "new situations added" as a reason to pay.
3. **Hold the AI decoder** until there are paying buyers and a rate-limit plan. Cost is tiny; ethics, abuse and support are the real costs.
4. **Skip** video course, community, Notion, audio for now.

### Concrete changes to the offer
- **Name the tiers by use.** The Playbook ($17) becomes the "Situation Playbook: find her text, send the reply." The Complete Pack ($27) adds Her Signals and the Reply Bank (PDF + Notes file).
- **Move the Reply Bank (Notes-app text file) into the $17 tier** or at least show it first. It is the lowest-risk, highest-convenience item; it also makes $17 look obviously worth it next to $7-9 template packs.
- **Delivery:** after the finder exists, the Polar purchase gives (a) PDF download, (b) license key for the finder, (c) Notes file.
- **Keep the launch price (first 100 copies) and the 30-day refund** from pricing-offer.md; they are consistent with this advice.

### Concrete changes to the sales page (site/index.html, not edited)
- `<meta description>` (line 7), `og:description` (line 15), JSON-LD Product description: replace "a 73-page texting playbook" with "find your exact texting situation in seconds: what her text usually means and the reply to send. 25 situations."
- Hero small line (line 265) "73 pages. Instant download." becomes "25 situations. Find yours in 10 seconds. Instant download." Line 382 "73 pages, set for your phone" becomes "25 situations, one tap from the index, set for your phone."
- Say the size once, low on the page, as reassurance ("two pages per situation, 2 minutes each"), never as the headline. The existing H2 "Twenty-five texts. Two pages each." already says it well; keep it.
- Add a three-step strip under the hero: **Tap her text. Read what it means. Copy the reply.**
- Add an FAQ: "Do I have to read all of it?" Answer: "No. Open the index, tap your situation, read two pages, send the reply."
- Show a screenshot of the index page and of one reply with a "copy" look. When the finder ships, a short screen recording of it.
- Do not claim outcomes ("get the date", "works"). Say what it does: reads her text honestly and gives you a clear, respectful reply.
- Per the show-before-ship rule, the owner approves the new hero and offer block before anything goes live.

### What would change my mind
- If the first 20 buyers use the PDF but ignore the index, test the finder sooner.
- If buyers ask for "paste her text," that is the signal to build the AI decoder.
- Track one number: **refunds / sales** over the first 30 days. Above ~10% means the promise on the page is wrong, not the price.
