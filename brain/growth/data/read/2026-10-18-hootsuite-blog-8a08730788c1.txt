Instagram verification is the process of getting a blue checkmark badge that appears next to your username on the platform. The verification badge signals to other users on the platform that an account really belongs to the user, artist, brand, or organization it represents. If you’re looking to enhance your presence while pursuing verification, consider brainstorming unique Instagram username ideas that reflect your brand identity. Your favorite celebrity probably has a blue checkmark on their account: Brands with a strong social media following usually have a verified badge too: But there are actually two types of verification: traditional verification and paid. Originally, verification was only available to “ notable and unique ” people or brands. The process of awarding that coveted blue checkmark was shrouded in mystery, and verification was usually restricted to celebrities and big brand names. These days, users can also pay for verification through Meta Verified . That means anyone can get a verified badge on their Instagram or Facebook accounts. Whichever route you take, the payoff is the same: Legitimacy and trust: The verification process requires a government-issued ID, so having a verified badge establishes your account authenticity and helps the community know it’s the real you. People are more likely to follow, message, and do business with an account they can confirm is genuine. Impersonation protection: Fake accounts exist for all kinds of reasons, from scams to parodies. A badge sets your account apart from impersonators, and Meta Verified adds proactive account monitoring to help protect your account from impersonators or hackers. Visibility and performance: Verified accounts appear at or near the top of comments and search results when people type in a business name. They also appear as a recommended business to follow in the Feed, which can translate into more followers, website clicks, and conversions. Extra features: Verified accounts get access to perks like exclusive stickers on Instagram Stories and Reels to help their content stand out. No matter which method an account used to get verified, the blue checkmark looks the same. That means it’s impossible to know whether it was awarded to a user through traditional means or if they’re paying for the Meta Verified service. Anyone can request a verified badge on Instagram. Though the requirements are slightly different for each type of verification. Traditional verification requirements If you want to go through the traditional verification process for your Instagram profile, you can apply for the blue badge if you’re a notable public figure, celebrity, or brand, and you meet the eligibility criteria. Instagram’s criteria is a bit vague, but here’s what we do know . In addition to following Instagram’s Terms of Use and Community Guidelines , your account also needs to be: Authentic: It must represent a real person, registered business, or entity. Unique: Only one account per person or business may be verified, with exceptions for language-specific accounts. Instagram generally doesn’t verify general interest accounts. Complete: Your account must be public and complete. That means you must have a bio, profile photo, and active account when applying. Notable: Your account must represent a well-known, highly searched-for person, brand, or entity. What counts as “notable” to one person may not be notable to another, which is why verification used to be out of reach for most Instagram users. But don’t despair. There’s now another option: Subscribing to Meta Verified. Meta Verified requirements Getting a blue tick through Meta Verified is much more simple. Here’s what you need to subscribe: Be at least 18 years old Be located in a country that offers Meta Verified Have a public or private profile that’s associated with your full name, aligns with naming standards, and has a profile picture that includes your face Meet minimum activity requirements, such as prior posting history A government-issued ID or driver’s license Two-factor authentication enabled on your profile Follow Instagram’s Terms of Use and Community Guidelines Meta Verified is also available for businesses globally, and the requirements and pricing differ from individual subscriptions. Business plans are sold in tiers, with higher tiers bundling extras like enhanced support and additional profile features. Pricing varies by country, currency, and whether you subscribe through the app or on the web, so check the price shown in your own account before committing. If you’re relatively confident you meet the eligibility requirements for traditional verification, or you’re ready to splurge on paid Meta Verified, read on. Traditional verification vs. Meta Verified: Which is right for you? The right path depends on how well-known your brand or name already is. Traditional verification is free but hard to get, while Meta Verified (the most costly social media subscription ) is open to almost anyone willing to pay a monthly fee. If not, Meta Verified is the faster and more realistic route. How to get verified on Instagram in 5 steps Want to try for traditional verification? Follow these five steps to request verification for your business account. Keep in mind you can only request traditional verification using the mobile app, not the desktop version. Go to your Instagram profile Tap the hamburger menu icon Tap Business tools and controls Tap Request verification Fill out the form and submit Step 1: Go to your Instagram profile First things first, open your Instagram app and make sure you’re logged into the account you want to verify. Then, tap your profile photo in the bottom right corner of the app to head to your Instagram profile. Step 2: Tap the hamburger menu icon From there, tap the hamburger menu icon (three parallel lines) in the top right corner of your profile. This takes you to all of your Instagram settings. Step 3: Tap business tools and controls Scroll down in the settings until you see the For professionals section. Then tap Business tools and controls . Step 4: Tap request verification Scroll to the bottom of this section to find the blue Request verification link and tap it. Step 5: Fill out the form Next, you’ll have to fill out the available form to request verification for your professional account. You’ll have to confirm both authenticity and notability with documentation. The information you’ll need to provide include: 1-2 identification documents for your business, like a driver’s license, passport, ID card, tax filing, utility bill, or articles of incorporation. The category and country of your notability, as well as any names that you or your business is known by. Additional social media accounts that showcase your notability. While many of the form fields are listed as “optional,” we recommend filling out as much as you can to increase your chances of receiving verification. Once you’ve completed the form, tap the Submit button at the bottom. After submitting, keep the following in mind: Just because you request verification doesn’t mean the platform will grant it. Submitting a verification request multiple times before getting a decision will cancel your application. Getting verified on Instagram doesn’t mean your Facebook Page will also receive verification, so you’ll have to submit a request on both platforms . Once verified, certain accounts (public figures, celebrities, and brands that meet specific account and eligibility requirements) may not change their Instagram username , so make sure you’re happy with the name you’ve chosen. If you’ve received verification using false or misleading information, Instagram reserves the right to take action, like removing your verified badge and/or disabling your account. How to get verified on Instagram with Meta Verified If you haven’t been able to get verified the traditional way or you don’t qualify, you might instead choose to go the Meta Verified route. With Meta Verified, individuals can pay from $11.99/month on the web (or $14.99/month when signing up through the iOS or Android app) to get the Instagram verification badge along with the other benefits that come with having a verified account. Businesses can subscribe too, with tiered plans that cost more and include extra features. Step 1: Navigate to verification settings If you have a business or creator account, get to the verification screen the same way you would for a traditional application: open your profile, tap the hamburger menu icon, tap Business tools and controls , then tap Request verification . The difference is that you won’t fill out the application form. Step 2: Subscribe to Meta Verified At the top of the verification request form, you’ll see a Before you start header that prompts users to sign up for Meta Verified if they aren’t a notable individual or prominent brand. Tap this to get started. Some accounts may have a prompt to join the waitlist before they can sign up. This may be the case for users in countries where the feature isn’t yet available, but many users say they’ve had to wait for a few weeks in the waitlist, even in applicable countries before getting access. Otherwise, you’ll immediately be hit with a prompt to Unlock benefits . Tap the button to move onto the next page. Here, you’ll see the price in your area along with some of the perks you’ll get access to. Tap Continue to move on. If you’re on iPhone, you’ll be prompted to confirm your subscription. You can also sign up on the Android app or the desktop version. Keep in mind that each platform is billed separately, so verifying both an Instagram and a Facebook account means two subscriptions. 6 tips to improve your chances of getting verified Ready to earn that blue check? A few ways to strengthen that footprint: Earn coverage in credible news outlets, trade publications, or industry podcasts Keep active, consistent profiles on your other major social platforms Make sure your website, press page, and business listings use the same name and branding Work toward a Google Knowledge Panel, which signals that your name is widely searched The more searchable your name or brand is off Instagram, the easier it is to prove notability on it. 6. Avoid third-party verification services Only Meta can grant an Instagram verification badge, so any service promising to get you verified for a fee is either selling you nothing or putting your account at risk. Many of these offers are phishing attempts designed to collect your login details or ID documents. Stick to the two legitimate paths: the in-app verification request form or a Meta Verified subscription purchased through Instagram, Facebook, or your app store. If an agency or DM claims to have an inside connection at Meta, treat it as a scam and report it. Common myths about Instagram verification A lot of bad advice circulates about how the blue checkmark works. Here are the misconceptions worth clearing up before you apply. Myth: You need 10,000 followers. Instagram doesn’t publish any minimum follower count for traditional verification. A small account that’s widely written about can qualify, while a large account nobody searches for may not. Myth: A third party can get you verified. No agency, growth service, or individual can grant verification. Only Meta decides, and paying an outside service usually means losing money or exposing your account credentials. Myth: Verified accounts get more algorithmic reach. The badge doesn’t boost how often your posts are shown in the Feed. It can improve how you appear in search and comments, which is visibility, not ranking. Myth: You can buy an already-verified account. Buying or transferring accounts violates Instagram’s Terms of Use and can get the account disabled, badge included. Myth: Verification is permanent. Instagram can remove a badge if you break its rules, change your account to something unrecognizable, or stop paying for Meta Verified. What to do if your Instagram verification request is denied A denial isn’t permanent, and you can apply again as soon as you receive a decision. Instagram doesn’t explain its reasoning, but most rejections come down to one of a few things: unclear or incomplete documentation, a profile that’s missing a bio or photo, a name that doesn’t match your ID or press coverage, or not enough evidence that you’re widely searched for. Here’s how to approach your next attempt: Wait for the decision, then reapply. Submitting again before you hear back cancels your application, so let the process finish first. Strengthen your documentation. Swap weak proof for official records like articles of incorporation, tax filings, or a government ID that exactly matches the name on your profile. Add better notability evidence. Fill in every optional field with links to recent news coverage, interviews, or verified profiles on other platforms. Tighten your profile. Make sure the account is public, complete, and actively posting before you try again. Consider Meta Verified. If notability is the sticking point, a subscription gets you the same badge without the approval hurdle. In short, treat a denial as a documentation problem first and a notability problem second, and decide whether the paid route is a better fit for where your brand is today.
