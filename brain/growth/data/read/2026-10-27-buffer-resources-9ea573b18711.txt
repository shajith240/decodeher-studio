The good news: Instagram has transparently shared a ton about the magical, majestic Instagram algorithm. (At Buffer, we’re big believers in transparency, so hat tip to Instagram for that!) The not-so-good news: Instagram has different algorithms for all parts of the app — namely feed, stories, explore, and reels. “ Does this mean I have to optimize my Instagram content four different ways to please the algo? ” Not really. The overarching signals — such as watch time, likes, and sends — are the same everywhere. And the principles behind them are evergreen enough to survive the small updates. Follow these, and you’re (mostly) golden. But still, knowing the specifics of how each subset of the algorithm works helps reach your goals faster. Reaching new people on Instagram works a tad differently from deepening relationships with the audience you already have, for instance. Here’s how each algorithm works, the evergreen principles behind all of them, and what to do about it. The short version If you didn’t already know, Instagram’s feeds are not in chronological order. The algorithm decides how to rank content. Why not chronological? Adam Mosseri explains ranking has two goals: help people find what interests them most grow the overall pie of reach so there's more to go around Without ranking, feeds would be overrun by the accounts that post most often, which tends to mean businesses and brands, and the average creator's reach would shrink. And Instagram doesn’t have one algorithm. It runs a separate ranking system for each part of the app (feed, stories, explore, and reels), because people use each one differently. Mosseri groups them into two general types: One ranks content from accounts people follow The other ranks content from accounts they don't follow (Instagram calls these recommendations) Underneath, they all work the same way. They predict how likely someone is to like, comment on, send or watch a post, turn those predictions into a relevancy score, and order posts by it. Recency matters too, which is why your feed is roughly, but not strictly, chronological. For creators, the split between the two types is the one to remember. The first decides whether your followers see you, and likes count slightly more there. The second decides whether anyone else does, and sends count slightly more. That's how new people find you. Ignore “hacks” that sound too good to be true. If you need to understand these principles more deeply and how to apply them, continue reading: 1. Originality “We want to create a platform that gives more distribution to the original content creator than to those who repost it,” Mosseri says. Instagram estimates how likely a post is to be original by looking at what the account has posted in the past, along with other signals, and Mosseri says the percentage of impressions going to original content has gone way up over time. This has been a long time coming: Mosseri announced Instagram will has been reducing reach for aggregator accounts since April 2026. So Instagram’s been doubling down on pushing original content for a while now. 2. Recency Instagram wants to keep up with the world as it happens. "It used to be that something might happen and you would see it on Instagram three, four, five days later, and that just doesn't work," Mosseri says. To fix that, Instagram shortened how long it takes to understand a new post and get it into ranking, especially for people who don't follow the account. The measure it tracks is the share of each day's impressions that go to posts less than 24 hours old, and Mosseri says that number has gone way up as well. 3. Breaking content This one is good news for smaller accounts. "We want to be a place where a new creator with a great idea can actually reach a massive audience," Mosseri says. Instagram does this by “auditioning” content. Auditioning just means showing posts from public accounts to some people who don't follow them, and if a post does well, the platform continues pushing that post to other people. That's how a post from a small public creator can reach hundreds of thousands, or even millions, of people. As someone who scrolls on Instagram way too much, I can confirm this is true: I see posts from new creators all the time in my feed now — it’s mixed with content from people I follow and other content Instagram thinks I might like. 4. Model size The last principle is about the system rather than your content. Instagram has made its AI models "larger and larger over the last few years in order just to get better at ranking overall," according to Mosseri. How the Instagram Feed algorithm works The Instagram Feed algorithm ranks three kinds of content: Posts from accounts that you follow Recommended posts that Instagram thinks you might like Ads You can find all content formats ranked on your Feed — videos, photos, and carousels . Instagram uses thousands of ranking factors (called 'signals') to organize your feed, prioritizing content that's most relevant to each person based on their interests and past interactions. Here are the most important signals, ranked by importance: What a user engages with: Posts a specific user likes, shares, saves, or comments on tell Instagram what content resonates with that person. Info about the content and its performance: This includes how many people like a post, how quickly people engage with it, plus more information about the post itself, like when it was posted and any location tags. Information about the person who posted: Instagram looks at how many people have interacted with the creator in the past few weeks. "This helps us get a sense for how interesting the person might be to you," says Instagram on their blog . Relationship history: Consistent interactions between accounts — like commenting on each other's posts — tell Instagram who might want to see more of your content. With all that information, Instagram makes a series of calculated guesses about how likely a user is to interact with a post, as Instagram says : "In Feed, the five interactions we look at most closely are how likely you are to spend a few seconds on a post, comment on it, like it, share it, and tap on the profile photo. The more likely you are to take an action, and the more heavily we weigh that action, the higher up in feed you'll see the post." How to improve your reach in the Instagram Feed All of these signals come down to whether people interact with your posts, so here's how to give them more reasons to. 1. Share carousel posts If you have multiple photos (or videos) that work well together, try sharing them as one carousel or even a few mini carousels. You can include up to 20 photos or videos in a single carousel. When a follower doesn't swipe through to the end, Instagram treats those unseen slides as "new content." The platform will show your carousel to that same person again later, picking up with the first slide that they didn't swipe to. This gives your carousel multiple chances to get engagement. This matches what we found in our analysis of more than 4 million Instagram posts : carousels earn around 109% more engagement per person reached than reels, and single images earn about 34% more than reels. 👉 Need more help? It's not a requirement, but as Mosseri points out , it can grab the attention of people who browse with sound on — and that can mean more engagement. Plus, adding audio to a post or carousel means it can also show up for users in the Instagram Reels feed. 🎵 Keep your audio fresh with the latest trending Instagram sounds . 3. When you follow Instagram's ideal image sizes , your feed posts will appear exactly as intended with no awkward cropping or pixelation issues. This keeps your content looking polished and gives it a better chance of getting engagement. How the Instagram Stories algorithm works The Instagram Stories algorithm ranks stories from accounts you already follow, putting the ones you're most likely to tap, reply to, and watch at the front of your tray. Stories (photos and videos that disappear 24 hours after posting) offer creators and businesses a way to appear at the top of followers' screens as soon as they open the app. Keep in mind that it can be tougher for brands or creators to show up here — Instagram sees stories as a place where people look for their closest friends. Here's what Instagram's Stories algorithm looks at: Viewing history: Instagram tracks how often someone views an account's stories to determine how high to place them in the stories tray. The more often someone watches your stories, the more likely they are to appear first. Engagement history: A user's interactions — such as sending a DM or liking stories — signal to Instagram that they're interested in seeing an account's stories. Relationship connections: Instagram considers how close someone is offline to a creator — such as friends or family — when considering where to place a story. Again, these signals will be used to "make a series of predictions about stories you'll find more relevant and valuable," Instagram says , "including how likely you are to tap into a story, reply to a story in DMs or move on to the next story — to determine which stories will be shown higher in your stories tray." Instagram Stories aren't typically the best way to reach new people — Mosseri has confirmed this too . Instead, think of stories as a way to build deeper connections with people who already follow you. They're perfect for building a community that keeps coming back. You can show up when followers open Instagram, learn more about them, and point them to your other platforms. How the Instagram Explore page algorithm works The Explore algorithm surfaces posts from accounts you don't follow, ranked mostly by how popular a post is and how much you've engaged with similar content. Here's where things get interesting for creators and brands looking to reach new audiences. The explore page was designed to help users find new accounts. In other words, it helps new audiences find you. This grid-style feed shows photos and videos you're likely to be interested in based on your past activity: posts you've liked, saved, shared, and commented on. Here are the signals that influence the Instagram Explore page algorithm. Information about the post: Like with feed, Instagram looks at how popular a post seems to be. How many people like, comment on, share, or save a post? And how quickly are they doing so? Post popularity signals matter more in the explore page than in feed or stories. A user's activity in explore: Instagram pays attention to what content a user has engaged with before, and how they've interacted with posts in the explore page in the past. The more someone engages with a certain type of post, the more likely they are to see similar content show up. A user's interaction history with the poster: Most content in the explore page comes from accounts people don't follow or haven't seen before. But if someone has interacted with an account before, Instagram sees that as a sign that they might be interested in more content from this account. Information about the person who posted: Here, Instagram looks at how often users have interacted with posts from an account in the past few weeks, to help users find "compelling content from a wide array of people," says Instagram . Here's an example from Instagram that I found really helpful in making sense of the explore page algorithm. "Let's say you've recently liked a number of photos from San Francisco's dumpling chef Cathay Bi ( @dumplingclubsf ). We then look at who else likes Cathay's photos, and then what other accounts those people are interested in. Maybe people who like Cathay are also into the SF dim sum spot @dragonbeaux . In that case, the next time you open explore, we might show you a photo or video from @dragonbeaux." How to get your posts in front of more people using the explore page Here are some tips to optimize your content for Instagram’s Explore page: 1. Use trending audio tracks Instagram pays attention to which audio tracks are taking off. When you add these popular sounds to your reels, photo posts, or carousels, your content has a better chance of reaching new people. You don't need to awkwardly force trending audio into everything you post, but it's worth picking a trending track when it naturally complements what you're creating. 2. Give people a reason to engage right away The explore page leans on how popular a post looks, including how quickly people like, comment, share or save it. A post that gets reactions fast has a better chance of being picked up. A few ways to make that easier: Put something save-worthy in the post. A checklist, a template or a how-to that people will want to come back to. Tell people what to do with it. A line like “save this for later” or “send this to someone who needs it” is enough. Reply to comments as they come in. In Buffer’s 2026 engagement report , Instagram posts where the account replied to comments earned 21% more engagement. That’s an association, and the data can’t show that replying caused it. 3. Track post shares Shares — especially DMs from people who don't follow you — count for more when Instagram is deciding whether to show a post to non-followers, Adam Mosseri explained in his podcast with Colin and Samir . When someone likes your content enough to send it to a friend, Instagram sees that as a strong signal of quality. Tap View insights below a post to see which posts are being shared the most. When you spot patterns in your most-shared content, you can focus on creating more of what people want to pass along to their friends. 💡 The explore page is also where you'll find Instagram's search function — which can be an important tool in your Instagram marketing kit. That makes the reels feed a particularly important area if you're looking to grow your audience . It also means the clearer your content is about what it's about (topics, niches, keywords), the easier it is for Instagram to match it with the right people. Instagram looks at: A user's activity: Instagram looks at what reels people have liked, saved, reshared, commented on, and engaged with recently. When someone engages with content similar to yours, they're more likely to see your reels. A user's interaction history with the poster: When someone engages with an account — whether through likes, shares, or comments — Instagram takes that as a sign that they want to see more content from that account, even if they aren't a follower. Information about the reel: Everything from the visuals to the use of trending or original audio, overall watch time (short, punchy clips tend to win), and early engagement signals influence how widely a reel is distributed across Instagram. Information about the person who posted the reel: Instagram looks at signals like a creator's follower count and engagement levels to share content from a mix of creators. Short-form video is also a big part of how Instagram connects people with their friends. Here’s what Adam Mosseri shared in his conversation with YouTube podcasters Colin and Samir : "Short-form video is so symbiotic with connecting people with their friends," Adam told Colin and Samir. "Part of our [Instagram's] reason to be is to connect people with friends." In other words: optimize your Instagram content to start conversations. Think about what makes your followers want to share your reel with someone they care about. Maybe it's relatable, helpful, funny, or thought-provoking. Every audience is different, so you'll need to experiment to find what works for yours. A viral reel shows Instagram that people are interested in your content, which increases the chances your next post gets seen too. 2. Did you know you can schedule trial reels with Buffer, too? 👀 How Instagram allows users to control their algorithm (and how it impacts your reach) Instagram launched Your Algorithm to allow users more control over what they see on their app. It shows an AI-generated summary of the topics Instagram thinks a viewer is interested in, and lets them ask for more or less of a topic, remove it, or add one Instagram has missed. It's live on reels, explore, and feed per Instagram's announcement . In part three of his ranking series , Mosseri said: "You can add topics you want to make sure that you never see anything from, and we're going to do our best to live up to those requests." What it means for your reach: When a post is recommended because of a viewer's chosen topic, Instagram labels it with that topic (for example, "From Running"). To get matched, name your topic in captions and on-screen text, and stick to one niche so Instagram knows who your content is for. Instagram hasn't said how much weight declared topics carry compared with what people actually watch, so treat them as one more signal, not a shortcut. Viewers also have three smaller tools to steer their feed. Favorites Viewers can add an account to Favorites from its profile. That account's posts then show at the top of their feed whenever it posts. What it means for your reach: A follower who favorites you sees everything you post, so it's the strongest vote a viewer can give you. It's worth asking your most engaged followers to add you. Following feed The following feed shows posts from accounts a viewer follows in chronological order, with no recommendations mixed in. What it means for your reach: only your followers can see you there, and because it's chronological, your reach in that feed depends on who follows you and when you post, not on ranking. Not interested Viewers can tap the three-dot menu on a post or reel and choose Not interested , and Instagram will show them less content like it. For a full fresh start, they can reset their recommendations entirely ( here's how ), which clears what Instagram has learned about them. What it means for your reach: a post that doesn't fit your niche is the one most likely to get tapped away, and it can teach Instagram to show that viewer less of your content. Staying on topic keeps your audience's signals working for you. When Instagram stops recommending content Instagram wants to keep potentially offensive content from appearing in users' feeds. To that end, it has published detailed recommendation guidelines for creators. Posts that go against these guidelines might not violate Instagram's Community Guidelines and are still allowed on Instagram, but they won't appear on the explore page or reels tab.
