TikTok lets you use AI, but requires creators to add a label when it’s used to create or significantly edit realistic-looking people or scenes. According to TikTok’s Community Guidelines , the policy covers two types of content: AI-generated content (AIGC): Any image, video, or audio made or altered by AI that depicts realistic-looking people or scenes. Significantly edited content: Media that’s been edited (with or without AI) to show someone saying or doing something they didn’t, or to make them unrecognizable. When you add a label to your TikTok video, a tag appears below your caption with text like “Creator labeled as AI-generated.” Forgetting to add an AI label may result in your post being removed or restricted by TikTok. Some content is off-limits, label or not. For example, TikTok bans AI content that could seriously mislead people about public issues or harm a real person. Here’s how each one works: Creator toggle: This is a label you add yourself. Add it when your post was completely generated or significantly edited by AI. Auto label: TikTok may add a label to your video automatically. It happens when you use TikTok AI effects, or when content from other tools carries Content Credentials (a type of metadata that shows how a file was made). Ad disclosure: Advertisers have their own AI label. If you’re running an ad made or heavily edited with AI, you need to turn on the “This ad contains AI-generated content” tag in TikTok Ads Manager. On TikTok, you need to label content that uses AI to show realistic-looking people or scenes. The key word is realistic . If your AI-generated content could pass for something real, there’s a good chance it needs a label. Small edits or touch-ups are a different story. Color-correcting or cropping a video, for example, are fine without disclosure. The same goes for everyday filters, like ones that add makeup. It protects your brand reputation Nobody wants a TikTok post to turn into a PR crisis because people felt tricked. How do you label AI-generated content on TikTok? To label AI-generated content on TikTok, turn on the AI-generated content setting before publishing an organic post, or apply an AI disclosure when creating a paid ad in TikTok Ads Manager. Here’s how to do both: 1. Organic content: Use the AI-generated content toggle For regular TikTok posts, you can add an AI label by following these steps in the TikTok app: Create or upload your video and tap Next Tap More options Toggle on the AI-generated content option Publish your post You can also disclose AI with a clear caption, sticker, or watermark. The toggle is just the quickest option. 2. Ads: Apply the required disclosure in the ad flow You can add an AI disclosure directly in TikTok Ads Manager. Here’s how: Open TikTok Ads Manager and create a campaign or go to an existing one Navigate to the Ad creation section Turn on the toggle that says This ad contains AI-generated content Finish setting up your ad and click Submit Once you submit, you can’t turn the toggle off unless you swap out the video or image. And if you duplicate a campaign, the toggle resets , even if you reuse an ad that was labeled before. Keep in mind: Spark Ads follow TikTok’s organic content disclosure process, so make sure the original post is properly labeled. What happens if you don’t disclose AI content on TikTok? If you don’t disclose AI content on TikTok, the platform may add a label for you, restrict its reach, or remove it altogether. It depends on how much harm the content could cause. Here’s how that might play out: TikTok adds a label for you: TikTok can identify some AI-generated content and add its own label. Once it’s there, you can’t remove it. Fewer people see your post: TikTok may limit your post’s reach, which means it won’t show up in as many feeds. Your post is still live, but it has a much harder time reaching anyone. Your post gets removed: If TikTok thinks your post could cause harm, it may remove it altogether. Your ad gets rejected: TikTok can reject or restrict ads with unlabeled AI content, putting your paid campaign on hold. The good news is that adding an AI label doesn’t hurt your reach by itself. So if AI played a major role in creating or editing your post, adding the label is the easy call. How can your team build an AI disclosure process? FAQ: TikTok AI disclosure policy What is TikTok’s AI disclosure policy? TikTok requires creators to label content that’s generated or significantly edited with artificial intelligence when it shows realistic-looking people or scenes. The policy also covers edited media that makes it seem like someone said or did something they didn’t, such as deepfakes. Under the Integrity and Authenticity section of its Community Guidelines , creators can disclose AI use with TikTok’s AI-generated content label, or with a clear caption, watermark, or sticker. Some content isn’t allowed even with a label, like manipulated media that misleads people about public issues or harms a real person. Why should creators label AI-generated content on TikTok? Creators should label AI-generated content on TikTok to avoid penalties like reduced visibility or content removal. Labels help viewers tell what’s real and what’s AI-generated, reducing the risk of confusion or misinformation. And for brands, being upfront about AI use is a lot easier than dealing with backlash after the fact. Does using an AI label affect your TikTok views or reach? No, TikTok says adding an AI-generated content label will not affect its distribution. That said, TikTok is also testing a setting that lets viewers see less AI content in their feeds, so labeled synthetic content may reach fewer people who choose to dial it down. Can you remove an AI-generated label on TikTok? No, TikTok says you can’t remove an AI label once the platform has applied it automatically. For labels added manually by creators, TikTok’s published guidance is less clear, so double-check your disclosure settings before you post. Does TikTok automatically detect and label AI-generated content? Yes, in some cases. TikTok uses automated detection to label content made with its own AI effects, plus content from other tools that carries Content Credentials, a type of metadata that shows how a file was made. Do I need to label AI-generated scripts or captions? No. TikTok’s policy covers images, video, and audio, so using AI to write a script or caption doesn’t require a label on its own. That changes if the script is read by an AI voice that sounds like a real person, or performed by an avatar with a synthetic face. How does Hootsuite help teams stay compliant on TikTok?
