Engagement rate is the percentage of your audience that interacts with a piece of content, rather than just scrolling past it. It’s calculated by dividing total interactions by a measure of audience size (usually followers, reach, or impressions), then multiplying by 100. What counts as an interaction depends on the platform, but it typically includes: Likes: The lowest-effort signal of interest Comments: A stronger signal, since they take real effort Shares and reposts: Content worth passing along to someone else Saves: Content people want to come back to Clicks: Link clicks, profile visits, or taps on your content DMs: Direct replies or message shares The key thing to remember is that engagement rate measures quality of attention relative to audience size, which is why a small account can easily outperform a much larger one. Download now Why does engagement rate matter? Engagement rate matters because it’s one of the few social media metrics that tells you whether your content actually connected with people. Follower counts and impressions measure exposure. Engagement measures response. For marketing leaders, tracking the social media engagement rate across accounts serves four practical purposes: Content quality signal: A dip in engagement often shows up before a dip in followers or conversions, making it an early warning system for content quality problems. Algorithm input: Most platforms use engagement signals to decide how widely to distribute a post, so higher engagement compounds into more reach. ROI indicator: Engagement rate normalizes for audience size, which makes it a fair way to compare performance across channels, regions, or business units. Strategy guide: Comparing engagement by format and topic shows you what to produce more of, and what to stop making. In short, engagement rate is the metric that tells you whether your audience cares, not just whether they saw it. How do you calculate engagement rate? You calculate engagement rate by dividing total engagements by an audience measure, then multiplying by 100. Which audience measure you use depends on what you’re trying to learn. There are three common methods: Engagement rate by reach, for judging content performance among people who saw it Engagement rate by followers, for benchmarking against industry averages Engagement rate by impressions, for paid campaigns and repeat exposure Here’s how each one works. For more variations, including daily and cost-per-engagement formulas, see our full guide to measuring social engagement . Engagement rate by reach Formula: (Total engagements / Reach) x 100 This is the most accurate measure of content quality, because it only counts people who actually saw the post. Use it when you want to know how compelling a piece of content was. Engagement rate by followers Formula: (Total engagements / Followers) x 100 This is the most widely reported engagement rate formula, and the one used for the industry benchmarks below. Use it when you need to compare your performance to published averages. Engagement rate by impressions Formula: (Total engagements / Impressions) x 100 Impressions count every view, including repeat views , so this number runs lower than engagement by reach. Use it for paid campaigns where frequency matters. You can find additional formula breakdowns if you need to go deeper. Note: If you’re calculating your account’s total engagement, include information about all your posts (e.g., total number of posts published, total number of likes, total number of impressions, and so on). If you’re calculating the engagement rate of a specific social media marketing campaign, only include the details of the social media posts that were part of the campaign. What is a good engagement rate? A good engagement rate generally falls between 1% and 5%, depending on the platform and industry. Anything above 4% is strong on Instagram and LinkedIn, while 1.5% to 3% is closer to normal on TikTok and Facebook. Here’s how the ranges break down per platform: Platform Low Average Good Exceptional Instagram Under 2.5% 4.2% 5% to 7% Above 7% LinkedIn Under 2% 3.9% 4.5% to 6% Above 6% Facebook Under 1.5% 2.9% 3.5% to 5% Above 5% X (Twitter) Under 1% 1.8% 2.5% to 4% Above 4% TikTok Under 1% 1.5% 2.5% to 4% Above 4% This is also why a question like “is 12% engagement on Instagram good?” has an easy answer. Yes, 12% is exceptional. It’s nearly three times the 4.2% average, and usually a sign of either a highly engaged niche audience or a post that broke out well beyond your follower base. Keep in mind that account size, content format, and industry all shift what’s realistic. Smaller accounts tend to post higher percentages simply because their audiences are more invested, so compare yourself to accounts of a similar size in your sector before deciding whether your number is a problem. How to improve your engagement rate Improving your engagement rate comes down to producing fewer, better posts for a clearly defined audience, then letting the data decide what you make next. Five approaches do most of the work: Audit your top posts first. Pull your 10 highest-engagement posts from the last quarter and look for the pattern in format, topic, and hook. Most teams find the answer is already in their own data. Cut volume, raise quality. Posting more often spreads engagement across more content without adding interactions. If your rate is falling while output is rising, that’s usually the cause. Ask for a response. Questions, polls, and prompts that invite a specific reply reliably lift comment volume. This is critical now that comments per post have fallen 16–24% across major platforms, and comments carry more algorithmic weight than likes. Post when your audience is actually online. Early interaction signals influence how far platform algorithms distribute a post, so timing has an outsized effect on the final number. Reply to comments quickly. Responses count as engagement and encourage more of it. Teams that treat comments as a channel rather than an inbox see measurably better rates. Benchmark against your own sector. Chasing a cross-industry average can send you after the wrong goal. Compare yourself to similar accounts in your industry and set targets from there. Done consistently, these shifts matter more than any single content trend, because they compound across every post you publish. FAQ: Average engagement rate What is a good engagement rate on social media? A good engagement rate on social media generally falls between 1% and 5%, depending on your industry and platform. Anything above 4% is strong on Instagram and LinkedIn, while 1.5% to 3% is closer to the norm on TikTok and Facebook. How is average engagement rate calculated across platforms? Average engagement rate is calculated across platforms using a basic engagement rate formula. Add up your total number of engagements on a piece of content (likes, comments, shares, and saves), divide by your number of followers, and multiply by 100. You can also swap out followers for another social media metric, such as impressions or reach. What factors impact average engagement rate for brands? The factors that impact a brand’s average engagement rate include its industry, audience size, chosen platforms, content formats, and posting consistency. Smaller accounts often see higher rates than large ones, since a smaller, more invested audience interacts at a higher proportion, and visual posts tend to outperform text-only posts. Platform algorithms also play a role in how far each post travels. How do engagement rate benchmarks vary by industry and platform? Engagement rates vary widely by both industry and platform. Construction, education, and nonprofits see the highest engagement rates on average. Platform-wise, Instagram and LinkedIn offer a higher engagement rate (around 4.2% and 3.9% overall) than Facebook and TikTok (around 2.9% and 1.5%). Does engagement rate or reach matter more? Whether engagement rate or reach matters more depends on your goal. Engagement rate is one of the most important metrics for content quality, while reach matters more when your goal is awareness and getting in front of new people. Tracking both helps you spot low engagement early and improve content performance. How can brands improve their engagement rate? Brands improve their engagement rate by creating high-quality content their target audience actually cares about, then doubling down on what works. Review your engagement data to see which content types are performing, boost top-performing posts with paid promotion, add hashtags to make content more discoverable, and routinely adjust your content strategy based on what the numbers tell you. What is a good engagement ratio? A good engagement ratio (also called engagement rate) generally falls between 1% and 5%, depending on the platform and industry. On Instagram, a ratio above 5% is considered good, while on Facebook, anything above 3.5% is a strong result. What is the 50/30/20 rule for social media? The 50/30/20 rule for social media is a content mix guideline suggesting that 50% of your posts should engage your audience, 30% should inform or educate, and 20% should promote your products or services. It’s a starting point rather than a law, and your own engagement data should guide how you adjust the balance. Is 12% engagement on Instagram good? Yes, a 12% engagement rate on Instagram is exceptional, well above the 4.2% average across industries. Rates that high usually come from smaller accounts with highly invested niche audiences, or from a single post that reached far beyond the existing follower base. How do you find your average engagement rate? You can find your average engagement rate by dividing total engagements (likes, comments, shares, saves) by your follower count, then multiplying by 100.
