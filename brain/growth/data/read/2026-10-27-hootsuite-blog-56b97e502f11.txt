Instagram’s Reels algorithm works by using AI to recommend videos it thinks you’re most likely to enjoy. To do this, it evaluates a large pool of potential Reels and ranks them based on how likely you are to engage with them. Instagram pays close attention to signals like: Watch time and completion rate: The primary signals Instagram uses to determine if viewers find your content valuable. Engagement signals: Likes, comments, and saves. Rewatches and shares: Whether people rewatch or share the video. Think beyond your industry Your audience isn’t only interested in your products or services. Creating Reels around adjacent topics, as long as they resonate with your audience, can help you reach new viewers. Since Reels are often shown to people who don’t already follow you, tapping into broader conversations on the platform is a smart way to expand your reach. Silvija Kemeraite , Social Media Manager at Omnisend , suggests thinking outside of your industry to engage your audience. “Don’t be scared to post content that’s not directly related to your business,” says Kemeraite. “This doesn’t mean completely ditching your area of business expertise, but always think about your audience and what would be interesting for them to watch. This can be especially helpful for brands in more “serious” industries like finance, education, healthcare, B2B, or nonprofits. 3. Use trending audio strategically Trending audio gives Instagram another signal to work with, and it can put your Reel in front of people already watching videos that use the same sound. Mosseri has pointed to audio and trending music as one of the simplest ways to improve reach. To find trending sounds, open the audio browser in the Reels editor and look for the upward arrow next to a track, or use a trend tracking tool to spot rising audio early. Save anything that fits your brand so you can act fast while the sound is still climbing. A practical rule: use trending audio for entertaining or community-focused Reels, and original audio or a voiceover for anything educational, where your explanation is the value. Either way, add captions. Plenty of people watch with the sound off. 4. Include clear calls to action Unlike other marketing channels that direct people elsewhere (like “visit the link in bio”), your Reels CTA should keep viewers engaged with the video itself. Saves and shares are strong signals to the algorithm that your content is resonating. The more people interact with your Reel, the more likely it is to reach new audiences. Your CTA can be as simple as “send this to a friend” or “leave a comment letting us know what you think.” Need inspiration? If you’re using trending audio or jumping on a trend, the content itself should take center stage. CTAs work best for educational or informative Reels, like when you’re sharing tips, walking users through a process, or showcasing your products. 5. Post consistently and at optimal times Consistency does more for Reels performance than volume. Publishing on a steady cadence each week gives Instagram more chances to learn who your content should reach. Timing matters too. Posting when your audience is already scrolling gives a Reel early engagement, which helps it get recommended more widely. You create an Instagram Reel in five steps from your mobile device: select or record a video, edit it, browse trending audio, add a cover, and publish or save as a draft. Step 1: select or record a video From your feed, tap the + button to create a new post. Then, select Reel from the options. From here, you can either hit the record button to create a video directly within the app, or choose a pre-made video from your camera roll. Reminder: You can upload a video up to three minutes long. Step 2: edit your video Next, you can use the built-in video editing tools to add layers, transitions, and effects to your recording. From the editing menu, you can: Cut out silences Add and rearrange multiple video clips Add voiceovers Adjust the speed Crop the clips Add audio or visual effects, like text and stickers Step 3: browse trending audio Explore curated sounds in the For You tab or see what’s trending. The green and red arrows let you know which sounds are gaining or losing popularity among users. You can also save audio to use later. Step 4: add or edit your Reels cover Your Reels cover image is what people see first when scrolling your profile. Pick an image that catches the eye and signals what the video is about. Either select a frame from your video to use as the cover, or upload a separate image from your camera roll. You can also preview how the cover will appear in your profile grid. Step 5: publish or save as a draft This is the final step. Review your Reel cover and make any edits before publishing or saving it as a draft. You’ll also need to add a caption. An AI caption generator can speed this up. Instagram Reels captions can be up to 2,200 characters long (the same as an Instagram post). You measure Instagram Reels performance using the insights attached to each Reel, plus a view across your whole account so you can compare formats. These are the metrics worth watching: Views and reach: Views count total plays, while reach counts unique accounts, which tells you how much of your audience is new. Watch time: Total time spent watching, and the clearest sign that your Reel held attention. Average watch time: How far the typical viewer gets before scrolling, which points straight at your hook and pacing. Replays: A strong signal for short, dense Reels, since rewatches count toward total watch time. Saves: Usually your best proxy for practical, evergreen value. Shares: The metric most tied to reach beyond your followers. Follows from a Reel: Shows whether discovery is turning into audience growth. Profile visits and link clicks: The closest in-app indicators of commercial intent. Instagram’s native insights are fine for a single Reel, but they make trends hard to spot.
