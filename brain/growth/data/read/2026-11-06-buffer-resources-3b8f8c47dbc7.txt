TITLE: 20 Social Media Metrics to Track (With Formulas and Benchmarks)

PUBLISHED: Thu, 05 Nov 2026 10:00:00 GMT

More doesn't necessarily mean better . Think emails in your inbox, glitter on a Mother's Day card, hot sauce… That applies to social media metrics, too. It's tempting to track everything your analytics dashboard throws at you. But not all social media metrics are created equal, and the ones worth your time come down to your specific social media marketing goals — whether that's testing a new format, growing an audience, or getting more people to your website. Metrics are most helpful when they guide your next move — adjusting your content, switching up your posting schedule, or even thinking up new products. In this guide, I'll walk you through 20 social media marketing metrics, what each one tells you, how to calculate it, and how to turn the right handful into KPIs. Key takeaways Social media metrics vs. KPIs: Metrics are everything you can measure on your accounts. KPIs are the three to five metrics you tie to a specific goal and target, such as lifting LinkedIn engagement rate from 4% to 6% in a quarter. Engagement rate is the most widely used metric for judging whether content is landing. The formula is total engagements ÷ reach × 100. Typical engagement rates differ by platform. Buffer’s analysis of 52+ million posts found 2025 medians of about 6.2% on LinkedIn, 5.6% on Facebook, 5.5% on Instagram, 4.6% on TikTok, 4.0% on Pinterest, 3.6% on Threads, and 2.5% on X. Reach vs. impressions: Reach counts the unique people who saw a post. Impressions, which many platforms now report as views, count every time it was shown, including repeats. Replying to comments is linked to higher engagement on every platform Buffer studied, with up to 42% more on Threads and 30% more on LinkedIn. Posting consistently protects your growth. In Buffer’s data, accounts that skipped a week of posting grew followers more slowly than their own usual rate. Vanity metrics like follower count and likes are most useful when you turn them into a rate, such as follower growth rate or engagement rate. Click-through rate, conversion rate, and return on ad spend are the metrics that connect social media activity to revenue. Social media metrics vs. social media KPIs You may hear people refer to metrics as "social media KPIs," or key performance indicators. They sound similar, but they aren't exactly the same. Metrics include every single thing you can measure on your social media channels. KPIs are the specific metrics you've chosen to track towards your goals, usually with a target attached. A metric becomes a KPI when you tie it to a goal and a number. A few examples: Goal: build awareness for a new product → KPI: grow monthly reach by 20% over the next quarter Goal: build a more engaged community on LinkedIn → KPI: lift average post engagement rate from 4% to 6% Goal: drive signups from social → KPI: 150 signups a month from social referral traffic You can track dozens of metrics, but you need only three to five KPIs to understand whether you’re close to or far from achieving your goals. Which social media metrics should you track? Start with the goal , not the metric. Then note where each one sits today before you set a target. A baseline makes “good” mean something for your account rather than someone else’s. Say your goal is getting more people to your website. You might pick click-through rate, social referral traffic, and conversion rate. If your posts currently average a 0.8% CTR and send 400 visitors a month, those are your baselines. A realistic target might be a 1.2% CTR and 600 visitors a month by the end of the quarter. Without the starting numbers, you wouldn’t know whether 1.2% was a win or a slip. Vanity metrics vs. actionable metrics Vanity metrics are numbers that look impressive but don't tell you what to do next — total follower count, likes on their own, or a single post's impressions. They aren't useless. Follower count still matters for creator partnerships, and impressions are a fair top-of-funnel signal. They just work better paired with a rate or an outcome: Follower count → follower growth rate Likes → engagement rate Impressions → click-through rate If a number can go up without changing anything you'd do differently, it's probably a vanity metric on its own. Social media engagement metrics Engagement metrics show how people are responding to your content — not just seeing it, but actively doing something about it. When you track these signals, you start to see clear patterns in what your audience enjoys and comes back for. 1. Engagement rate Formula: Engagement rate = total engagements ÷ reach × 100 Engagement rate tells you how much your audience is interacting with your content. Things like likes, comments, shares, or saves all count towards engagement rate. It's a quick way to understand whether your content is catching people's attention and prompting them to respond. Some tools divide by followers instead of reach (useful for comparing accounts of different sizes), and LinkedIn includes clicks in its own engagement count. Whichever version you use, stick with it so your numbers stay comparable over time. Freelance social media marketer Rebecca Broad uses this metric as a gut check while creating content. "Keeping engagement rate in mind means I constantly assess visuals and copy and ask 'why would anyone care?'" says Rebecca. "Why would someone who sees this post stop scrolling and spend precious time and energy engaging with it?" It's a perspective that can help you keep your audience at the center of every creative decision. What's a good engagement rate? It depends heavily on the platform. Post engagement rate Post engagement rate shows you which posts are making people stop and react — not just scroll by. This type of social media engagement also tells the algorithm your content is worth sharing more widely, though engagement types are often weighted slightly differently on each platform. For example, shares (especially in DMs) are a strong signal to the Instagram algorithm . Meaningful comments carry weight on LinkedIn , especially when they get a conversation going. Buffer Insights shows each post’s engagement rate for you, so you can spot patterns without exporting everything to a spreadsheet and working it out by hand. Track this metric when: you're experimenting with new content formats you're promoting a message you want to stand out you want to keep a general eye on what your audience responds to How to use post engagement metrics When you see a spike in engagement, don't stop at the number — figure out what's behind it. Was the post tied to a trending topic? Did you ask a question that got people talking? Did the visual pop in the feed? Those are the kinds of clues that can guide what you post next. And if something really clicks, look for ways to repurpose it elsewhere — Rebecca often reworks strong LinkedIn posts into blog CTAs (calls to action). On the flip side, if engagement is low, that's your cue to experiment. Change up the format, try a different tone, or shift your focus and see what sticks. Campaign engagement rate Campaign engagement rate shows how an entire social media campaign is landing — messaging, post timing , and creatives together. Because campaigns often span multiple formats and run over several days, this metric can reveal patterns you may not spot while looking at individual social media posts alone. In Buffer, you can use tags to segment posts related to a specific campaign and then see its engagement rate with one filter. Track this metric when: you're launching a new product or service you're running a seasonal promotion ( Black Friday + Cyber Monday , anyone?) you're raising awareness on a topic How to use campaign engagement metrics For brands, campaign engagement rate is a helpful way to adjust your approach mid-campaign. If engagement's low, you might rethink your next post or shift your ad spend. It's also a practical way to test influencer or creator partnerships before making bigger commitments in future campaigns. Creators can include strong campaign engagement rates in pitches and media kits . A solid campaign engagement rate shows brands that you won't just get them in front of people — you'll get people talking, following, and maybe even buying. Platform engagement rate Platform engagement rate gives you a broader view of how your content is performing across an entire social media platform. When you track this over time, you'll start to see whether your audience is still responding or if engagement is beginning to dip. Buffer shows the overall engagement rate for each channel you connect, and you can set a custom date range to see how it changed over a specific period, such as before and after a strategy shift. Track this metric when: you're evaluating whether your overall social strategy is working or needs a shift you want to know if the platform is still worth your time, or if your focus might be better placed elsewhere How to use platform engagement metrics If engagement is steady or trending up, it's a sign that the platform is worth continued time and effort. If it starts to fall, it could mean your content needs a refresh, your posting rhythm needs tweaking, or your audience is moving elsewhere — and it might be worth following them. Before you pack up, check the platform-wide trend in the table above: Instagram's median engagement rate fell around 26% between 2024 and 2025 across Buffer's whole dataset, so a dip there isn't necessarily about you. 2. Comments Formula : Total number of comments per post (also evaluate the quality of engagement) Comments count the replies people leave on your posts. They take more effort than a like, which makes them one of the clearest signs that a post gave someone something to say. Comments are also the engagement you can do the most about. In Buffer's analysis of nearly two million posts , posts where the account replied to comments earned more engagement than the same account's posts without replies: +42% on Threads +30% on LinkedIn +21% on Instagram (That's a correlation rather than proof of cause, but it held on all platforms studied.) Track this metric when: you want to know which topics start conversations, not just get a quick tap you're building a community and want to see if people talk to each other, not just to you you're testing questions or prompts in your captions How to use comments Judge comments by quality, not quantity. Are people asking questions about your content? Are they sharing their own experience with the topic? A handful of thoughtful comments tells you more than a hundred fire emojis. Replying is how you turn comments into more engagement, and into a community. Buffer pulls comments from all your connected channels into one place, so you can reply without opening each app (and getting pulled into scrolling while you’re there). And when a comment sparks an idea, you can turn it straight into a new post from inside Buffer (instead of scrambling to note it down somewhere so you can remember it). 3. Shares Formula: Amplification rate = shares ÷ followers × 100 Shares (also called reposts or retweets) count how many times people pass your content on to their own followers or send it to someone directly. A share puts your content in front of people who don't follow you yet, which is why platforms treat it as a strong signal. If you want a rate rather than a raw count, use the amplification rate. Track this metric when: you want to grow beyond your existing audience you're testing whether a piece of content is useful or relatable enough to pass on you're comparing which formats travel furthest How to use shares Look at what your most-shared posts have in common. Often it's a clear takeaway, a strong opinion, or something people want to send to a specific friend or colleague. Make more of that, and make it easy to share — a single, self-contained point travels further than a post that needs context. To spot patterns faster, ask your AI assistant (connected to Buffer through MCP ) “Which of my posts got the most shares, and what do they have in common?” 4. Saves Formula: Save rate = saves ÷ reach × 100 Saves count how many people bookmarked your post to come back to later. They're available on Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, Pinterest, and X (as bookmarks), though not every platform shows them publicly. A save means someone found the post useful enough to keep, which is why saves tend to follow tutorials, checklists, and reference-style posts. Track this metric when: you're creating educational or how-to content you want to know which posts have long-term value, not just a quick reaction you're testing carousels or other formats designed to be revisited How to use saves Saves are one of the best sources of content ideas you have. Each save tells you that someone wanted to come back to that post, so look at your most-saved posts and ask what made them worth keeping. Was it a step-by-step process, a checklist, a set of examples? Then build on it. A post that explained one tip can become a series covering the rest. A popular checklist can become a downloadable template. And if the same topic keeps pulling saves, read the comments on those posts too. Follow-up questions there are often your next post, already written for you by your audience. 5. Average watch time and completion rate Formulas: Average watch time = total watch time ÷ total views Completion rate = completed views ÷ total views × 100 Average watch time (sometimes called average view duration) is the number of seconds people spend watching your video. Completion rate is the share of viewers who watched to the end. These are two of the clearest video performance metrics — a higher average watch time means viewers are sticking around for longer. They matter even more on platforms like Instagram and TikTok , where watch time influences what gets recommended or shown to more people. Depending on the platform, you might be able to track average watch time for your full library or just individual videos. YouTube gives you both, while Instagram Reels focuses on video-by-video performance. Track this metric when: you're experimenting with different video lengths or formats you're comparing video content performance across platforms you want to see which topics or styles keep people watching How to use video engagement metrics A longer average watch time suggests your content is working, whether it’s the pacing, the topic, or how the video opens. Use that to guide what you make next. If people are dropping off early, open the retention graph for that video. Most platforms show one in their analytics, including YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram Reels. Look at where the line drops, not just how far: A steep drop in the first three seconds means the hook isn’t working. The topic may be fine, but the opening didn’t give people a reason to stay. Rework the first line or first frame before you change anything else. Buffer’s free Hook Generator can give you a few options to test. A sudden dip in the middle usually lines up with a slow section, a long intro, or a point where the video stops delivering what the hook promised. Cut or tighten that part next time. A gradual slope to the end is normal. Compare your completion rate across similar videos to see which lengths your audience sticks with. Reach and awareness metrics Reach and awareness metrics tell you how often your posts are being shown and how far they're traveling. Content can't make an impact if no one's seeing it. 6. Reach Reach tells you how many different people saw your post or ad. Each person is counted once, whether they saw that post one time or five times. If a post has a reach of 15,000, that means 15,000 different people saw it. It's one of the key metrics that Hailley Griffis , Director of Communications and Content at Buffer, tracks. "Different communities and social networks will engage differently, but if you are reaching an increased number of people, then you're doing something right," says Hailley. Reach helps you compare which formats, topics, or platforms are getting seen the most. Track reach when: you're testing different formats to see which ones grab more attention you want to understand which topics or messages your audience cares most about you want to see if posting at different times or on different days helps you reach more people you're reviewing which platforms your audience is most active on How to use reach Just like you did with engagement rate, start by checking which posts had the highest reach, then dig into the context. Was it the topic? The timing? Did someone share it and extend your audience? Hailley reviews her reach every couple of weeks to find these patterns and make small adjustments that add up over time. One thing she's noticed: showing up regularly makes a difference. "My reach continues to increase on Threads the more I stay consistent," says Hailley. "I've spotted the same pattern for other creators too. While my Threads engagement is still growing, I've also seen increases in overall reach in the last few months of maintaining consistency." Buffer’s data shows a similar pattern for growth. Across 4.8 million channel-weeks, accounts that skipped a week grew followers more slowly than their own baseline. 7. Impressions and views Impressions count how many times your content is shown on someone's screen, even if it's to the same person more than once. For example, say someone taps through to an Instagram post from your stories and then also sees the post in their feed the next time they open the app. This counts as two impressions, even though it's the same person. It's easy to confuse impressions with reach, but they measure different things. Reach tracks how many unique people saw your posts. Impressions count all views, including repeat ones. 💡 Impressions or views? Several platforms now report "views" rather than impressions. Instagram has made views its primary success metric, and TikTok and YouTube have always led with them. The idea is the same — total times your content was seen — but each platform counts a view slightly differently, so compare views within a platform rather than across them. For Meryoli Arias , Head of Social Media at Glide, impressions are a north star metric. "As a social media marketer, my main focus is top-of-mind relevance. And the number of times my message and brand are in front of X amount of people (impressions) helps me with that," she says. Track impressions when: you're testing or comparing content formats you're checking if the algorithm is showing people your posts you're measuring overall brand awareness Just keep in mind: a post can rack up thousands — even millions — of impressions without leading to more followers or engagement, or meeting your business objectives. "I had a viral post that hit 2 million impressions. Something that took me two minutes to create was being appreciated by so many people," says Meryoli. But despite its success, the post didn't do much to boost any of Meryoli's business goals, like increasing click-throughs or sign-ups. "I learned that virality is not and should never be the goal of social media. A good tap in the shoulder for the ego does not equal business success." In Buffer, you can see a post’s reach and impressions side by side (for channels that support both). That makes it easy to spot which posts reached lots of new people and which ones were mostly seen again by the same people. If impressions are well above reach, the post was shown to the same people multiple times. If the two are close, most views came from different people. How to use impressions Look at which formats, topics, or post styles get the most impressions, then tweak your formats, posting schedule, or content collaborations to build on what's working. If your impressions start to slide, check three things before you overhaul your content: How often you’re posting. Impressions are closely tied to how much you post. In Buffer’s LinkedIn data , moving from one post a week to two to five meant about 1,182 more impressions per post. And in our Instagram analysis , weeks with no posts at all pulled follower growth below an account’s usual baseline. Which formats you’re using. Some formats are built for reach. On Instagram, Reels get around 36% more reach than carousels , even though carousels earn more engagement. Whether it’s just you. Check the platform-wide trends in the engagement rate table above. If a whole platform is down, the dip may say more about the platform than about your content. ⚡ Posting less than you meant to? Set a weekly posting goal for each channel in Buffer, and your queue will show you when you’re falling behind. They help you see who's in the proverbial room, so you know who to talk to in your content. 8. Follower growth rate Formula: Follower growth rate = new followers in a period ÷ followers at the start of the period × 100 Follower growth rate tracks how quickly new people are choosing to follow your account over time. Follower count is one of the most visible social media metrics, and often one of the first numbers people look at when trying to measure progress — the growth rate turns it into something you can compare month to month. If you started the month with 2,000 followers and gained 150, your follower growth rate is 7.5%. For Meryoli, it's another north star metric. She uses follower growth to see whether her content is reaching new people and growing her audience — a sign that it's drawing attention and building momentum. Buffer Insights tracks follower growth for every channel you connect and compares it with the previous period, so you can see straight away whether this month’s growth sped up or slowed down. Track this metric when: you want to see if your content or campaigns are reaching fresh eyes you're measuring the impact of a recent launch, collaboration, or experiment you're setting goals for overall brand awareness or community growth How to use follower growth Follower growth can be a helpful KPI, but it's most meaningful when paired with metrics like engagement and conversions. A rising follower count on its own doesn't say much about whether those followers are taking action. That's why it's sometimes called a "vanity metric" — it reflects potential reach, not impact. Use it as a signal, not the final measure of social media success. If your follower count is going up but comments, likes, or clicks aren't, it might be time to adjust your content or CTAs to better engage your followers or reach the right audience. 9. Audience demographics Audience demographics show you what kinds of people make up your audience — details like age, gender, and location. It's a simple way to check whether you're attracting your target audience. You can use that information to create more content for the people already following you and adjust your social media strategy if you need to reach someone else. Track this metric when: you want to know if you're attracting your target audience you're considering a shift in content style or topics and want to check fit you're preparing a brand or investor pitch or media kit that needs audience proof How to use demographics Start by comparing who’s actually following you with who you want to reach. If they match, keep doing what you’re doing, and use the data to fine-tune your tone, visuals, and timing. If they don’t, your content is probably speaking to the wrong people. Say you sell project management software to team leads in their 30s and 40s, but most of your followers are students aged 18 to 24. That’s usually a sign your posts lean towards general productivity tips rather than the problems team leads deal with every day. Shift some of your posts to the questions and frustrations your ideal audience has, like running meetings, managing workloads, or reporting to leadership. Then check your demographics again in a month or two to see whether the mix is moving. If you’re a creator, demographic data can also strengthen your pitch to brands. When you show that your audience lines up with who they’re trying to reach, you become a much more appealing partner. Brand health metrics Brand health metrics show whether — and how — people talk about your brand, which helps you see what's working and where there's room to improve. Social media monitoring and listening tools make it easy to track those conversations and see how people feel. 10. Brand mentions Formula : Total mentions in a set period Brand mentions measure how often your brand name pops up in posts, captions, or comments, whether someone tags your @handle or just types your name. Not every mention comes with a tag, so if you're just relying on notifications, you're likely missing pieces of the bigger conversation.
